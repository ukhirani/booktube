## 1. Embedding-similarity candidate generation

- Pairwise comparison of n items is O(n^2). Blocking ("candidate selection") is the standard first stage of entity resolution: it "restricts comparisons to entity pairs that are more likely to match" so matching becomes tractable; filtering further prunes pairs below a similarity threshold before verification. — Papadakis et al., *A Survey of Blocking and Filtering Techniques for Entity Resolution* (ACM CSUR, 2019) https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.06167.pdf
- Concrete scale math: linking two 100,000-record databases = 10^10 pair comparisons, while the maximum number of true matches grows only linearly with corpus size; approximate blocking trades a little recall for large efficiency gains. — Christen, *Performance and scalability of fast blocking techniques for deduplication and data linkage* (VLDB 2007) https://users.cecs.anu.edu.au/~Peter.Christen/publications/vldb2007blocking.pdf
- Blocking recall/precision is governed by blocking-key choice and block-size limits: if no shared key survives, the true match is never compared, and a miss in candidate generation is unrecoverable downstream. — Papadakis et al. (above); also *Comparative Analysis of Approximate Blocking Techniques for Entity Resolution* (PVLDB 2016) https://helios2.mi.parisdescartes.fr/~themisp/publications/pvldb16-blockingtechniques.pdf
- Embedding retrieval as candidate generation works, but recall@k is finite and improves slowly with k: for ontology-concept matching, retrieval recall averaged 82.09% at top-5, 84.66% at top-10, 86.82% at top-20 (text-embedding-ada / sentence-BERT / SPECTER2 retrievers). — Vu et al., *LLMs4OM: LLMs for Ontology Matching* (2024) https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.10317
- Production record linkage is routinely framed as k-NN retrieval over embeddings (cosine via FAISS backend) with an explicit **no-match threshold**, because "not all entities in the query need to have a match in the key." — *LinkTransformer* (ACL 2024 demo) https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-demos.21.pdf
- Similarity scores are comparable only under a fixed normalization: Voyage embeddings are unit-normalized so cosine == dot product == reversed Euclidean ranking. — Voyage AI docs https://docs.voyageai.com/docs/faq
- ANN indexes (HNSW/FAISS class) make the retrieval step itself cheap; the cost and risk live in what is done with the candidate pairs afterward (see §5, §6).

## 2. LLM verification / confirmation passes

- Two-stage "LLM as filter, LLM as expander" for KG entity matching (Gemma-3 27B; OAEI and Gollum datasets): the **filter step has a low false-negative rate** and is "highly reliable when rejecting a pair... less reliable when accepting one"; the **expansion step achieved precision 0.13** because the LLM "forces a selection even when no correct match exists" in the candidate list. Correct-in-top-10 rate was 49% (60% when the right entity ranked 1st). — *Using LLM to Improve Knowledge Graph Entity Matching* (CEUR Vol-4085, 2025) https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-4085/paper32.pdf
- LLM+retriever pipelines can match or beat specialist ontology matchers, but unevenly: across 20 OAEI tasks, LLMs4OM beat OAEI-2023 systems on some (e.g. MI-EMMO F1 94.30) and collapsed on others (SNOMED-FMA F1 25.64 vs 78.50 for the traditional matcher; MI-MatOnto 32.97). The retriever stage bounds the LLM (top-5 recall ~82%). — Vu et al. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.10317
- LLMs hallucinate in matching work. *OAEI-LLM* defines a taxonomy of LLM alignment hallucinations: **missing** mappings, **incorrect** mappings, and **disputed** mappings (where the LLM may be right and the reference wrong). — Wang et al., *OAEI-LLM: A Benchmark Dataset for Understanding Large Language Model Hallucinations in Ontology Matching* (arXiv 2024) https://arxiv.org/html/2409.14038v1
- Cost-aware LLM verification exists as a research problem: BoostER selects which pairs to ask an LLM under a token budget by expected entropy reduction (NP-hard; greedy (1-1/e)-approximation), models LLM accuracy Theta explicitly (e.g. 90%) so imperfect answers still sharpen the match distribution, and prompts for yes/no plus a confidence in [0.5, 1.0], treating scores <0.5 as unusable. — *On Leveraging Large Language Models for Enhancing Entity Resolution* (WWW '24 Companion) https://arxiv.org/html/2401.03426v1
- Naive all-pairs LLM scoring is uneconomic: "a comparatively tiny database with just one thousand entities can yield a million (1k x 1k) candidate pairs, translating to thousands of dollars in inference costs." — *Learning from Natural Language Explanations for Generalizable Entity Matching* (EMNLP 2024) https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.352.pdf
- Domain shift hurts: fine-tuning LLMs for entity matching raises in-domain F1 but "hurts cross-domain transfer"; small models distilled with LLM-generated explanations generalize better (up to +10.85% F1 out-of-domain). — *Fine-tuning Large Language Models for Entity Matching* (arXiv 2024) https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.08185
- Specialist matchers already compose LLM/embedding calls with other techniques: Matcha uses sentence-BERT (all-MiniLM-L6-v2) label embeddings + cosine as one algorithm among many; OntoMatch does graph-neighbourhood candidate selection at threshold 0.4, zero-shot LLM prompting, a 0.95 high-precision matcher, and Hopcroft-Karp 1:1 enforcement. — OAEI 2024 system papers: Matcha https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-3897/oaei2024_paper3.pdf ; OntoMatch https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-3897/oaei2024_paper5.pdf

## 3. Ontology / entity-linking methods

- OAEI is the standing evaluation campaign for ontology alignment (13 tracks, 13 participants in 2024), reporting precision/recall/F1, Hits@K/MRR, and a **rejection-rate** metric "to examine if systems can reject all plausible mappings for entities that actually have no alignment" — NIL handling is an explicit evaluation dimension, not an afterthought. — *Results of the Ontology Alignment Evaluation Initiative 2024* https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-3897/oaei2024_paper0.pdf
- Exact name equality is a deceptively strong baseline: on the OAEI conference track StringEquiv reached precision 0.997 / recall 0.766 / F1 0.622, while the best full matchers (Matcha F1 0.941, LogMapBio 0.888) beat it only via lexical+structural+LM ensembles. — OAEI 2024 results https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-3897/oaei2024_paper0.pdf ; https://oaei.ontologymatching.org/2024/results/conference/
- Matchers emit **confidence values**, and measured precision/recall shifts materially with reference-alignment style (sharp vs discrete vs crowd-consensus continuous): in 2024, five of seven conference-track systems emitted only 1.0 confidence, while LogMap and Matcha emitted varied confidences. — OAEI 2024 conference results https://oaei.ontologymatching.org/2024/results/conference/
- Logical post-checks are part of the evaluation: systems are scored on **consistency and conservativity violations** of the merged ontology; in 2024 only 3 of 7 conference-track tools had no consistency violations and all 7 had conservativity violations (some flagged "violations" were themselves false positives of the checker). — same URL
- Cross-KG entity-alignment failure modes transfer directly to cross-book concept alignment: long-tail items carry too little structural signal for structure-based methods; **in YAGO-3, 34% of entities share a name with another entity** (e.g., 20 "Paris"es); benchmarks unrealistically assume a 1:1 match for every entity, so real systems need a learned **NIL threshold** on distance-to-nearest-target for genuinely unmatched items. — Shimizu et al., *An Experimental Study of State-of-the-Art Entity Alignment Approaches* (TKDE 2022) https://www.computer.org/csdl/journal/tk/5555/01/09174835/1myqEh9yLwA
- Embedding-based alignment depends heavily on seed quality; iterative bootstrapping (BootEA-style) "inevitably generates incorrect marks" that then mislead training — mitigations in the literature are mutual-nearest-neighbor + threshold gates and alignment editing. — *KG embedding methods for entity alignment: an experimental review* (Data Mining and Knowledge Discovery, 2023) https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10618-023-00941-9 ; *Knowledge graph entity alignment research based on representation learning* (AI Review, 2024) https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10462-024-10866-4
- Fully automatic ontology matching is described as "slowly reach[ing] an upper bound" on alignment quality — the stated reason the OAEI interactive track exists. — OAEI interactive track https://oaei.ontologymatching.org/2024/results/interactive/

## 4. Confidence calibration and thresholding

- Modern neural confidence is systematically overconfident: measured expected calibration error (ECE) typically 4-10% across vision/NLP models; a single scalar **temperature scaling** step fit on validation NLL is "surprisingly effective" (often beating more general Platt variants) and leaves accuracy unchanged. — Guo et al., *On Calibration of Modern Neural Networks* (ICML 2017) https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/guo17a.html
- Distribution-free guarantee option: **conformal prediction** converts any score into a prediction set with coverage >= 1-alpha under exchangeability, no distributional assumptions; practical guidance is ~1,000 calibration points for stable 1-alpha +/- epsilon coverage; per-input (conditional) coverage is impossible in general — only marginal coverage is guaranteed. — Angelopoulos & Bates, *A Gentle Introduction to Conformal Prediction* https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.07511 ; Shafer, *An Introduction to Conformal Prediction* https://arxiv.org/pdf/0706.3188
- Thresholds are model- and corpus-specific: Matcha's evaluators note an "optimal threshold could be set to 0.9, which would capture most true positives"; CANARD swept thresholds 0.5-1.0 in 0.1 steps plus 0.8/0.85/0.9 for stage two (1,800 parameter combinations); OntoMatch uses 0.4 candidate / 0.95 matcher gates. — Matcha https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-3897/oaei2024_paper3.pdf ; CANARD https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-3897/oaei2024_paper9.pdf ; OntoMatch https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-3897/oaei2024_paper5.pdf
- Precedent for calibrated fact probabilities at web scale: Google **Knowledge Vault** stores each triple with a probability the authors report as "well calibrated"; fusing multiple extractors **plus graph-derived priors** grew >0.9-confidence facts from ~100M to 271M and visibly shrank the uncertain [0.3, 0.7] band — priors cut false positives. — Dong et al., *Knowledge Vault: A Web-Scale Approach to Probabilistic Knowledge Fusion* (KDD 2014) https://lunadong.com/publication/kv_kdd.pdf
- KG-construction best practice: every extracted fact should carry confidence from the extractor, confidence from the source, and provenance of the source, stored **as edge metadata**, before knowledge alignment, fusion, and consistency checking. — Benatallah et al., *Uncertainty Management in the Construction of Knowledge Graphs: a Survey* (2024) https://arxiv.org/html/2405.16929v2

## 5. Cost per library

- **Embeddings (OpenAI):** text-embedding-3-small $0.02 / 1M tokens; text-embedding-3-large $0.13 / 1M tokens. — https://platform.openai.com/docs/pricing
- **Cheap chat tiers:** Anthropic Claude Haiku 4.5 $1 input / $5 output per 1M tokens (batch 50% off: $0.50 / $2.50); OpenAI gpt-5-nano $0.05 / $0.40 per 1M; Gemini 2.5 Flash-Lite $0.10 / $0.40 per 1M (Gemini 3.x Flash-Lite $0.25 / $1.50). — https://docs.anthropic.com/en/docs/about-claude/pricing ; https://platform.openai.com/docs/pricing ; https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing
- **Mid-tier chat:** Anthropic Claude Sonnet 5 $2 / $10 per 1M (batch $1 / $5); Claude Opus 5.5 $4 / $20 (batch $2 / $10). — Anthropic pricing page (above)
- **Embedding cost arithmetic** (arithmetic, not a source claim): a 100k-word book is roughly 130k tokens, so embedding full text at $0.02/MTok is about $0.003/book; a 1,000-book library is about 130M tokens ~= $2.7 (small) or ~$17 (large). Embedding the whole corpus is negligible next to any LLM stage.
- **LLM verification cost arithmetic** (arithmetic): a pair-verification call with ~600 input + 50 output tokens at Haiku 4.5 list price is ~$0.00085 (batch ~$0.00043). A library with 25k concepts x top-10 candidates = 250k pair verifications is roughly $210 list / $105 batch — i.e. order $10^2 per library for one verification sweep at cheap-tier prices; ordering at Sonnet-class prices is ~4-8x that.
- **Full extraction-style KG building (first-party):** Microsoft's GraphRAG cost post measured ~$0.34 per 30,000-word document (GPT-4-turbo-era rates), of which extraction is ~58% of indexing tokens and extraction+summarization ~90% of build cost; embeddings for a 37k-word book were ~$0.0056. — Microsoft Tech Community, *GraphRAG Costs Explained* https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/azure-ai-foundry-blog/graphrag-costs-explained-what-you-need-to-know/4207978
- **Secondary cost figure:** one 2026 practitioner write-up puts end-to-end GraphRAG over a 500-page corpus at roughly $50-200 (model-dependent), with automatic triple extraction dominating. — https://tianpan.co/blog/2026-04-06-graphrag-at-10k-scale (secondary source — verify against first-party rates before budgeting)
- **Cost levers:** batch APIs (Anthropic/OpenAI 50% off), prompt caching (Anthropic/OpenAI/Gemini all price cached input well below fresh input; Gemini also charges hourly storage), and asking only uncertain pairs (BoostER, §2).

## 6. Cost of false links

- Wrong edges compound through feedback loops: recommendation/graph pipelines trained on their own outputs show popularity-bias amplification (items that appear more are ranked higher), which measurably **declines aggregate diversity**, shifts taste toward popularity, and homogenizes users — with the strongest negative effect for users in minority groups. — Mansoury et al., *Feedback Loop and Bias Amplification in Recommender Systems* (CIKM 2020) https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3459637.3482160
- Longitudinal simulation: individual diversity of users **decreases over time regardless of adoption**, and system-wide diversity peaks early then declines — i.e. errors do not wash out, they accumulate. — *Analysing the Impact of User Adoption and Consumption Patterns on Diversity in Recommender Systems* https://arxiv.org/html/2602.16315v1 (2026 preprint, secondary evidence)
- A concrete diversity study found niche users' diversity deteriorates the most after adoption of a recommender, and that the effect persists over time. — Ribeiro et al., *How Welcome is Personalization? Analyzing the Impact of a Recommender System on Long-Tail Consumption* (Group & Recommender Systems, 2025) https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-99053-6_15
- Hallucinated edges enter as if verified: KG-construction surveys list hallucination as a core threat, including mapping text spans to **nonexistent entities** and merging/splitting the wrong entities, with errors then propagated downstream. — Gao et al., *A Comprehensive Survey of Knowledge Graph Construction with Large Language Models* (2025, arXiv 2510.20345) https://arxiv.org/html/2510.20345v1 ; Benatallah et al. (arXiv 2405.16929, section 4)
- Wrong edges poison paths: a practitioner GraphRAG analysis notes that one hallucinated or wrong relation distorts every path and community summary built on top of it, and calls hallucinated triples "the biggest hidden risk" of automated KG building — secondary source. — tianpan.co (above)
- In record linkage terms, false positives are the cheaper error class in blocking filters but the expensive one after linking, because they create fabricated similarity that retrieval then keeps surfacing (see feedback-loop findings above); NIL/duplicate-name evidence is in section 3.

## 7. User correction and severable-link patterns

- **Wikidata precedent for severable links:** every statement triple has a rank (preferred / normal / deprecated); **deprecated rank means the statement is known to be wrong or inappropriate but is kept, not deleted**, and it is excluded from ordinary queries by default but retrievable with a qualifier flag. Statements also carry references (provenance) per claim. — Wikidata:Help:Ranking https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Help:Ranking
- **Provenance standard:** W3C PROV-DM defines an interoperable model for provenance (entity/activity/agent, wasGeneratedBy/wasDerivedFrom/wasAssociatedWith), usable to record how a link was created and by what process — the standard mechanism for attaching "how we know this" to an edge. — W3C Recommendation https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-dm/
- Interactive alignment works but needs careful UX: OAEI interactive-track results show a few hundred expert decisions move F1 substantially (e.g. ALIN anatomy 0.835 -> 0.929 from top to bottom of the table; conference track 0.65 -> 0.79; large-lexicon 0.951 -> 0.967), and in one study 30% simulated user error still matched fully-automatic results while lower error rates beat them. — OAEI 2024 interactive results https://oaei.ontologymatching.org/2024/results/interactive/ ; Jimenez-Ruiz et al. (cited therein)
- Human input degrades fast with noise: with a 10% user error rate interactive matching can become **worse than fully automatic** (observed in the anatomy track), and expert knowledge is "expensive and scarce" for mapping production ontologies. — same results page ; *ALIN* system paper https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-3897/oaei2024_paper4.pdf
- User correction of links over time has a published precedent in search/entity linking literature: disambiguation and correction signals are treated as ground-truth updates, and Wikidata's deprecation flow (above) is the mature example of "soft" unlinking without data loss.

## 8. Automatic KG construction from document sets: precedents and failure modes

**Precedents**

- **Knowledge Vault (Google, 2014)** — the reference web-scale precedent: extraction from ~500M web documents into 1.6B candidate triples; probabilities are calibrated after extractor fusion with graph priors; facts carry source counts, extraction confidence, and graph-derived confidence as first-class data. — Dong et al., KDD 2014 https://lunadong.com/publication/kv_kdd.pdf
- **GraphRAG (Microsoft, 2024+)** — source-document-to-kg + local/global search pattern, with first-party guidance on cost; explicitly warns that **fully incremental updates of source text can drift** and recommends versioning/scatter-gather rebuilds, and that community summaries need regeneration after updates. — Microsoft GraphRAG docs and cost post: https://microsoft.github.io/graphrag/ ; https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/azure-ai-foundry-blog/graphrag-costs-explained-what-you-need-to-know/4207978
- **OAEI** (section 3) and **entity-alignment surveys** (section 3) are the evaluation precedents for the linking step specifically.

**Failure modes documented in the literature**

- **Hallucination of edges/entities** — LLM extraction maps spans to nonexistent entities or merges wrong ones; OAEI-LLM shows the same problem for alignment links (missing / incorrect / disputed). — Gao et al. 2025 https://arxiv.org/html/2510.20345v1 ; Wang et al. 2024 https://arxiv.org/html/2409.14038v1
- **Entity duplication / inconsistent identity across sources** — different documents describing the same real-world entity create duplicate nodes unless a linking stage reconciles them; fuzzy joins and key mismatches are called out as graph-quality problems. — *A Practical Guide to Building a Knowledge Graph* (InfoQ 2025, secondary) https://www.infoq.com/articles/knowledge-graph-practical-guide/
- **Schema drift and pipeline fragmentation** — multi-stage LLM KG pipelines accumulate errors as extraction, entity normalization, and integration occur in separate stages; the survey names "pipeline fragmentation" and "cumulative error propagation" as core challenges, plus non-determinism across runs. — Gao et al. 2025 https://arxiv.org/html/2510.20345v1
- **Noisy training signal** — OpenIE-style extractors are trained on weakly labeled data with no consensus, so downstream benchmarks inherit that noise; paired with the fact that LLM explanations/scores are poorly calibrated without post-hoc work (section 4). — Christen et al., *When to Use What: Extracting and Comparing Data about Entities from Text* (ACL 2023 Findings) https://aclanthology.org/2023.findings-acl.490 ; Guo et al. 2017
- **Cost and update economics** — extraction dominates cost (section 5); incremental updates risk drift and summary regeneration (GraphRAG, above).
- **Evaluation gap** — KG construction lacks one canonical evaluation framework; surveys recommend explicitly modeling extraction/learning/evaluation separately with confidence + provenance (section 4). — MDPI survey (2025) https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/15/7/3727 ; Benatallah et al. 2024 https://arxiv.org/html/2405.16929v2

## Implications for the concept-alignment decision

Decision-relevant facts only (no method recommendation; the method choice stays on #12):

1. **Candidate generation is unavoidable and lossy.** All-pairs comparison is quadratic (10^10 pairs at just two 100k-record sets), so every production system blocks or retrieves first; embedding retrieval tops out around 82% recall@5 / 87% recall@20 on concept-matching benchmarks — whatever runs after it cannot recover pairs the retriever missed (Papadakis 2019; Christen 2007; LLMs4OM 2024).
2. **Exact name match is a strong, cheap floor, and name collisions are the known failure.** StringEquiv alone hits precision 0.997 / F1 0.622 on OAEI conference; but 34% of YAGO-3 entities share a name with another entity, so labels alone will mis-link polysemous concepts ("Paris", "attention", "revolution") and an explicit no-match/NIL path is required by both OAEI evaluation and TKDE 2022.
3. **LLM verification is asymmetric: trustworthy for rejection, weak for acceptance.** Measured: low false-negative filtering, expansion precision 0.13 under forced choice, correct-in-top-10 only 49%; OAEI-LLM documents missing/incorrect/disputed hallucination classes; one domain collapses to F1 25.64 vs 78.50 for the traditional matcher (CEUR 2025; arXiv 2409.14038; LLMs4OM).
4. **Confidences need calibration before any threshold means anything.** Modern networks show 4-10% ECE out of the box; temperature scaling fixes most of it; conformal prediction gives a distribution-free 1-alpha coverage guarantee at ~1,000 calibration points; published matcher thresholds (0.4/0.9/0.95) are model- and corpus-specific — a threshold transplanted from another model is not calibrated (Guo 2017; Angelopoulos & Bates; OAEI 2024 system papers).
5. **The budget shape is: embeddings negligible, verification sweep ~$10^2 per library, full extraction ~$0.34 per 30k-word doc.** At 2026-09-26 cheap-tier prices, ~250k pair verifications is ~$105-210 (batch/list); batch, caching, and ask-only-uncertain-pairs are the standard levers (OpenAI/Anthropic/Gemini pricing pages; BoostER; Microsoft cost post).
6. **False links do not stay local.** Feedback-loop studies show wrong edges amplify popularity, reduce aggregate and individual diversity over time, and hurt niche/minority users most; automated KG extraction is explicitly warned to propagate hallucinated edges downstream (Mansoury CIKM 2020; arXiv 2602.16315; Gao 2025).
7. **A severable, provenance-bearing link already has standards precedent — no invention needed.** Wikidata's deprecated-but-kept rank (excluded from default queries, still retrievable) plus W3C PROV-DM give the pattern for "link with confidence + provenance, soft-retract later without deleting history"; the KG uncertainty survey prescribes storing confidence and provenance as edge metadata before fusion.
8. **Human correction is high-leverage and noise-sensitive.** A few hundred expert decisions move F1 by ~0.05-0.09 in OAEI interactive results, but ~10-30% user error erases the gain — correction UIs must be low-friction and wrong approvals must be cheap to undo (OAEI 2024 interactive; ALIN).
9. **Automatic KG construction from documents is precedented but ships known failure modes.** Knowledge Vault (1.6B triples, calibrated fusion) and GraphRAG (cost-documented, with update-drift/versioning guidance) are the working precedents; the named failure modes are hallucinated edges, duplicate entities, schema drift, and cumulative error across fragmented pipeline stages (KDD 2014; Microsoft 2024+; Gao 2025).
