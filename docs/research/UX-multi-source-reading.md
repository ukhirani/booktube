# UX — Multi-source and cross-book reading precedents

- **Ticket:** ukhirani/booktube#32 (Wayfinder `wayfinder:research`)
- **Branch:** `research/UX` · **Date of research:** 2026-09-26
- **Method:** external factual research against primary sources (product documentation, design write-ups, controlled studies, accessibility standards). Every material claim carries a source URL. This file surfaces facts only — no design recommendations; the design decisions stay on #12 (multi-book scope) and #27 (reader prototype).

## 1. Sources panels and in-context citation access

- **NotebookLM is the clearest product precedent for a dedicated sources panel.** Sources live in a "Sources" panel where each source has a checkbox to include or exclude it from the next answer, and clicking `+ Add or unselect` toggles sources in the panel ([Google NotebookLM Help, "Create a notebook"](https://support.google.com/notebooklm/answer/16206563?hl=en)). With 5+ sources, NotebookLM auto-labels and categorizes them into user-editable labels ([same source](https://support.google.com/notebooklm/answer/16206563?hl=en)).
- **NotebookLM's citation interaction is two-stage: hover shows the exact quoted text immediately; selecting the citation navigates to the quote's location in the source** "so you can easily view it in context" ([Google NotebookLM Help, "Use chat"](https://support.google.com/notebooklm/answer/16179559?hl=en)). Google positions this as the trust mechanism: "See the source, not just the answer … clear citations … showing you the exact quotes from your sources" ([notebooklm.google](http://notebooklm.google/)).
- **NotebookLM limits its mobile app's citation feature set** — the Help pages carry an explicit warning that "the NotebookLM mobile app may have limitations to this feature" ([Google NotebookLM Help](https://support.google.com/notebooklm/answer/16179559?hl=en)).
- **Controlled evidence on where sources are shown exists.** He & Liu ran a between-subjects experiment (N=372) comparing four source presentations for cited AI text: Collapsible list (low visibility, low accessibility), Footer (high visibility, low accessibility), Hover Card (low visibility, high accessibility), and a persistent sidebar whose cards are vertically aligned to their in-text citations (high visibility, high accessibility) ([arXiv:2601.14611](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.14611)).
- **In that experiment, the aligned sidebar uniquely held up as citation density rose**: "as citation density increased, Sidebar users demonstrated significantly higher critical thinking and synthesis scores compared to other conditions", while hover cards "facilitated seamless, on-demand verification during the task" ([arXiv:2601.14611](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.14611)).
- **End-of-message footers decayed.** Citation usage in the Footer condition "declined sharply after the fourth message"; the authors attribute this to "increasing interface friction of accessing citations buried in previous message footers", not to disengagement (writing time stayed constant) ([arXiv:2601.14611](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.14611)).
- **People skim citations rather than open them.** Across all four interfaces, hovering (exploration) dominated clicking (full-text verification); click rates for full-text verification stayed below 1 per round and uniform across groups ([arXiv:2601.14611](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.14611)). Footer and sidebar list layouts produced "batch processing" of citations (Explore→Explore self-loops of 30.0% and 32.4% of interaction budget) versus 19.2% for the collapsible list ([arXiv:2601.14611](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.14611)).
- **Annotation tools pair an inline highlight layer with a positional sidebar.** Hypothesis's client has an "annotator" injected into the page that paints highlights, plus an iframe sidebar of annotation cards; hovering a card emphasizes its highlight and clicking a highlight focuses its card ([Hypothesis, "Overview of the Hypothesis system"](https://web.hypothes.is/help/overview-of-the-hypothesis-system)). In LMS deployments the sidebar sits "to the right of the reading" and annotations are ordered by "the portion of the text each student is annotating, making it easy to connect the annotation directly to the text" ([Hypothesis, "Getting Started with Blackboard"](https://web.hypothes.is/getting-started-with-blackboard)).
- **Hypothesis also models anchor failure explicitly:** when a passage changes too much for the fuzzy-anchor algorithm to re-find, the annotation is not hidden — it moves to a dedicated "orphans" tab, with the lost anchor text struck through on the card ([Hypothesis blog, "Showing Orphaned Annotations"](https://web.hypothes.is/blog/showing-orphaned-annotations); [help page](https://web.hypothes.is/help/what-are-orphans-and-where-are-they)).
- **Wikipedia's layout convention puts a single "Notes and references" appendix after the body and after "See also"**, i.e. provenance is deferred to one terminal section, with inline superscript markers as the only in-text trace ([Wikipedia Manual of Style/Layout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style/Layout)).

## 2. Per-claim attribution to different works

- **scite classifies every individual citation statement, not just the citing paper.** Its "Smart Citations" show the citation context from the citing article, the section it appears in (Introduction, Methods, Discussion…), and a supporting/contrasting/mentioning classification ([scite blog, "Citations in Context"](https://scite.ai/blog/citations-in-context-from-scite); [scite features](https://scite.ai/features)). Its API returns tallies per DOI plus the citing papers and the section of each statement ([scite API docs, Smart Citations](https://docs.scite.ai/guides/smart-citations)).
- **Per-statement classifiers are imperfect and this is documented.** An independent evaluation of scite found human assessors disagreed with its labels for a large share of statements — of 96 citations scite called "mentioning", assessors judged 40 better classified as supporting and 17 as contrasting (["Evaluating the Accuracy of scite", reported in scite's own report page](https://scite.ai/reports/scite-a-smart-citation-index-1ZNdYEAM)).
- **NotebookLM binds each citation to a verbatim quote plus a resolvable location** (hover → quote, click → jump to passage) rather than to a document-level pointer ([Google NotebookLM Help](https://support.google.com/notebooklm/answer/16179559?hl=en)).
- **Shortform attributes commentary to other works inline, inside the summary.** Its published previews carry "(Shortform note: …)" paragraphs that cite a different book or a paper mid-claim — e.g. the *Atomic Habits* summary links *Outliers* by Malcolm Gladwell and a 2009 Ordóñez et al. paper (with DOI) inside the summary flow, and its homepage preview explicitly sets one author's model against Duhigg's from *The Power of Habit* ([Shortform *Atomic Habits* summary page](https://www.shortform.com/summary/atomic-habits-summary-james-clear); [Shortform homepage](https://www.shortform.com/)).
- **Readwise's data model keeps provenance attached to every highlight.** A highlight record carries `text`, `title`, `author`, `book_id`, `location`, `location_type` (page/location/offset/time_offset/order), `url`, and `highlighted_at` ([Readwise API documentation](https://readwise.io/api_deets)); in Reader, highlights and notes are themselves documents with a `parent_id` pointing at the article/book they belong to ([Readwise Reader API](https://readwise.io/reader_api)).

## 3. Side-by-side versus interleaved comparison

- **The comparison-visualization literature classifies all comparison designs into three building blocks: juxtaposition (side-by-side), superposition (overlaid), and explicit encoding (a new view that states the relationship, e.g. the difference).** This taxonomy comes from Gleicher et al.'s survey of 100+ comparison papers ([Gleicher et al., "Visual comparison for information visualization", Information Visualization 10(4), 2011](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1473871611416549); restated in the systematic review [L'Yi, Jo & Seo, "Comparative Layouts Revisited"](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.00192)).
- **Fragmented layouts have a documented cost.** The related-work section of the source-transparency experiment states that "fragmented layouts and split attention reduce comprehension and integration, even when all relevant information is technically available" ([arXiv:2601.14611](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.14611), citing McClure & Clink 2009, Lee et al. 2024, Min et al. 2025).
- **Spatial alignment of sources to their citations is testable and was engineered deliberately.** In the N=372 study the sidebar condition used bounded isotonic regression to position source cards so each card aligns vertically with its in-text citation, prioritising viewport-centre visibility and first occurrences ([arXiv:2601.14611](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.14611)).
- **Sequence evidence on verification vs. flow:** floating (hover) cards let users "seamlessly verify and insert a citation, then immediately resume", whereas footer and sidebar list layouts showed more transitions from Cite back to Explore, i.e. list-based layouts pull readers into continued source-searching rather than back to the text ([arXiv:2601.14611](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.14611)).
- **Multi-column layouts are not a free comparison win.** Eye-tracking and online-reading studies find mixed results: no significant comprehension differences across 1/2/3-column layouts in several experiments, with reading speed sensitive to column count × justification ([Baker 2005, Wichita SOAR](https://soar.wichita.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/90254df5-57a5-4d17-ac4c-d7309ffb1b4e/content); [Dyson 1997](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0097849397000484)).
- **The classic cross-book comparison method is passage-level, not book-level.** Adler & Van Doren's syntopical reading requires "bringing the authors to terms" with a neutral terminology and framing neutral propositions so that passages from many books can be compared against one set of questions ([Adler & Van Doren, *How to Read a Book*, 1972, pp. 316–333 (archive.org PDF)](https://ia902804.us.archive.org/19/items/howtoreadabook1972edition/How%20to%20Read%20a%20Book%20-%201972%20Edition.pdf)).

## 4. "Also discussed in…" and related-concept navigation

- **Wikipedia's "See also" is the canonical related-concept list, with hard limits.** It is defined as "a useful way to organize internal links to related or comparable articles and to encourage readers to explore related concepts", but it is optional, must be "relevant and limited to a reasonable number", and "as a general rule … should not repeat links that appear in the article's body"; entries get a short annotation when relevance isn't apparent ([Wikipedia Manual of Style/Layout § "See also"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style/Layout)).
- **Wikipedia also supports point-of-need related links inside the flow:** the `{{See also}}` and `{{Further}}` hatnotes are placed immediately under a section heading to point at a related or fuller treatment, provided they don't duplicate a body wikilink ([Wikipedia Manual of Style/Layout § Headings and sections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style/Layout)).
- **Hover previews make related-concept navigation cheap, with measured behavioural effects.** Wikipedia's Page Previews show a short summary + image on link hover and also preview a footnote on hovering its marker ([MediaWiki, Page Previews](https://www.mediawiki.org/wiki/Page_Previews)). Three 2016 A/B tests (Hungarian, Italian, Russian) found previews "increase the precision with which users select the pages they read, reduce the cost of exploration … and allow users to selectively focus on a single topic"; pageviews fell while "the number of distinct pages that readers interact with" rose ([MediaWiki, Page Previews](https://www.mediawiki.org/wiki/Page_Previews)). The 2017–18 English/German deployment measured roughly a 4% pageview drop ([arXiv:1903.10670](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1903.10670)).
- **Citation hover previews are a separate, default-on feature.** Reference Tooltips show the full footnote in a tooltip on hovering a superscript citation, default delay 200 ms, configurable to hover or click, user-disableable, JavaScript-only ([MediaWiki, Reference Tooltips](https://www.mediawiki.org/wiki/Reference_Tooltips); overview at [Wikipedia:Tooltips](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Tooltips)).
- **Academic "related work" navigation runs on co-citation, not just links.** Connected Papers builds a force-directed graph where "papers are arranged according to their similarity … even papers that do not directly cite each other can be strongly connected", using co-citation and bibliographic coupling over ~50,000 papers around an origin paper, and explicitly states "Connected Papers is not a citation tree" ([Connected Papers, About](https://www.connectedpapers.com/about)).
- **scite's related-concept navigation is the citation-statement view:** a report lets readers "read citation statements from each citing paper to see how an article and its findings have been discussed or used, find co-citations, filter by citation types (supporting, contrasting, and mentioning)" ([scite blog, arXiv collaboration](https://scite.ai/blog/citations-in-context-from-scite); [scite features](https://scite.ai/features)).

## 5. Reading across multiple books on one theme

- **Syntopical (comparative) reading is a documented method with a defined sequence.** Adler: "When reading syntopically, the reader reads many books, not just one, and places them in relation to one another and to a subject about which they all revolve… mere comparison of texts is not enough"; the steps run from collecting relevant passages, to constructing neutral terminology, to framing neutral propositions, to defining the issues and ordering the passages by issue ([Adler & Van Doren 1972, Part IV (archive.org PDF)](https://ia902804.us.archive.org/19/items/howtoreadabook1972edition/How%20to%20Read%20a%20Book%20-%201972%20Edition.pdf)).
- **Reading multiple texts measurably beats re-reading one.** A 2024 experiment found that at a one-week delay, multiple-text reading produced better knowledge-test performance than re-reading a single text, and both beat single reading; the authors frame this as learners needing to "build interconnections … not only within a single text but across multiple texts" ([Instructional Science, "Comparing the effectiveness of multiple text reading and rereading"](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11251-024-09686-4)).
- **Shortform's product pitch is explicitly cross-book:** summaries are structured to show how one book "fits into the broader field", e.g. comparing Clear's four-stage habit model against Duhigg's three-stage model, and reviews note summaries "tie together related books" ([Shortform homepage](https://www.shortform.com/); third-party observation in [Wonder Tools](https://wondertools.substack.com/p/shortform)).
- **Readwise's premise is cross-source resurfacing:** highlights from "over 20 sources" (Kindle, iBooks, Pocket, PDFs, web) are unified in one library and resurfaced via Daily Review ([Readwise Reader](https://readwise.io/read); [Readwise docs](https://docs.readwise.io/readwise)).
- **NotebookLM's unit of multi-source reading is the notebook:** "a collection of sources for a specific project", with each notebook independent of the others ([Google NotebookLM Help](https://support.google.com/notebooklm/answer/16206563?hl=en)).

## 6. Showing provenance without overwhelming readers

- **Defaults and escape hatches matter: Wikipedia ships reference tooltips on by default, with a 200 ms delay, hover *or* click mode, and a user-facing on/off** (cookie for anonymous users, preference tab for logged-in users) ([MediaWiki, Reference Tooltips](https://www.mediawiki.org/wiki/Reference_Tooltips)).
- **Previews reduce exploration cost without forcing navigation:** the stated design goal of Page Previews is that users "gain context … without navigating away from their original topic" ([Wikimedia Foundation, Page Previews documentation roundup](https://wikimediafoundation.org/news/2018/05/09/page-previews-documentation)).
- **Density has a measured ceiling effect.** In the N=372 study, transparency/trust/satisfaction did not monotonically improve with more visible sources — the sidebar condition scored *lower* than collapsible on perceived transparency/usefulness in the regression while scoring *higher* on actual critical thinking as density rose ([arXiv:2601.14611](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.14611)).
- **Explanations can misfire:** the same literature reports that "providing more information does not necessarily improve understanding, and can sometimes increase misplaced confidence or shallow verification" ([arXiv:2601.14611](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.14611), citing Mitchell et al. 2019, Bordt et al. 2022).
- **Quote-vs-commentary separation has a consumer precedent in Shortform's labelled inline notes** — "(Shortform note: …)" paragraphs are visually and verbally marked as additions, distinct from the book's own claims, even when they contradict them ([Shortform *Atomic Habits* summary](https://www.shortform.com/summary/atomic-habits-summary-james-clear)).
- **scite's report shows provenance as filtered lists rather than in-line clutter:** readers filter citation statements by type, section, year, or source type before reading them ([scite features](https://scite.ai/features)).

## 7. Mobile considerations for citation-heavy reading

- **Hover does not exist on touch, and Wikipedia documents this openly.** The Reference Tooltips docs note the feature "requires JavaScript" and "may not be usable with tablets and other touch screen interfaces without eliminating the current 'click takes you to the reference list' behavior" ([MediaWiki, Reference Tooltips/en](https://www.mediawiki.org/wiki/Reference_Tooltips/en)).
- **Wikimedia's mobile reference design is a bottom overlay, not a tooltip:** the reference "overlay slides up from the bottom of the page", occupies "a maximum of 50% of the bottom of the screen" with interior scrolling, stays until dismissed, and the page scrolls so the tapped marker remains visible above it ([MediaWiki, Mobile design/Reference reveal](https://www.mediawiki.org/wiki/Mobile_design/Reference_reveal) — page marked obsolete/archival but is the documented design rationale).
- **Readwise gates marginalia on width:** annotations "will appear in the right margin, just like traditional marginalia, provided your screen has enough width" ([Readwise blog, "The Next Chapter of Reader"](https://blog.readwise.io/the-next-chapter-of-reader-public-beta)) — i.e. side-by-side annotation is a desktop layout, with a fallback implied for narrow screens.
- **Mobile-first content guidance warns against fixed pixel sizes and side-by-side elements:** Wikimedia's editor guidance recommends vertical stacking as "a safer alternative" to paired images and testing any CSS property ≥100 px on a real device; over 50% of Wikimedia traffic is mobile web ([MediaWiki, Recommendations for mobile friendly articles](https://www.mediawiki.org/wiki/Recommendations_for_mobile_friendly_articles_on_Wikimedia_wikis)).
- **NotebookLM explicitly ships a reduced citation feature set on mobile** (§1).

## 8. Accessibility considerations for citation-heavy reading

- **Hover/focus content is regulated by WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.13 Content on Hover or Focus (AA).** Any citation card shown on hover must be *dismissible* (e.g. Esc, without moving the pointer), *hoverable* (pointer can move onto it without it vanishing), and *persistent* (stays until trigger removed/dismissed); the Understanding doc names "custom tooltips, sub-menus, and other nonmodal popups" as the covered cases and notes hover-triggered content "should also be able to be triggered by keyboard focus" per SC 2.1.1 ([W3C, Understanding SC 1.4.13](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/content-on-hover-or-focus)).
- **Overlays must not strand keyboard focus:** WCAG 2.2 added SC 2.4.11 Focus Not Obscured (Minimum) at AA — the focused element must not be entirely hidden by author-created content ([W3C, WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22)).
- **Small citation chips must meet a size floor:** WCAG 2.2 SC 2.5.8 Target Size (Minimum) (AA) requires pointer targets of at least 24×24 CSS pixels (with inline exceptions) ([W3C, WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22)).
- **Any two-pane (side-by-side) source layout must reflow:** SC 1.4.10 Reflow (AA) requires content to work at 320 CSS px width without two-dimensional scrolling — which single-column reference stacks satisfy natively ([W3C, WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22)).
- **Colour alone cannot mark quotes, chips, or provenance:** SC 1.4.1 Use of Color (A) requires colour not be the only visual means of conveying information; SC 2.4.4/2.4.9 require link purpose to be determinable ([W3C, WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22)).
- **Accessible footnote practice on the W3C lists favours native semantics plus bidirectional links:** recommendations were to "stick with native html elements (ol, li, etc.), and to include links going to and from the footnote or endnote reference" with a heading for the notes section; Digital Publishing ARIA 1.0 defines `doc-footnote`/`doc-endnotes` roles whose AT support should be tested ([w3c-wai-ig thread, Peter Weil 2021-07-30](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/w3c-wai-ig/2021JulSep/0111.html); [Bryan Garaventa reply, 2021-07-30](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/w3c-wai-ig/2021JulSep/0117.html), citing [DPUB-ARIA 1.0](https://www.w3.org/TR/dpub-aria-1.0/)).
- **`aria-describedby` on a footnote link can announce the note on focus in Chrome/Firefox with JAWS/NVDA but not Safari/iOS** — where the link instead "still makes sense in context when swiping left and right with one finger" ([w3c-wai-ig thread, Bryan Garaventa 2021-07-30](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/w3c-wai-ig/2021JulSep/0117.html)).
- **Screen-reader text is a stream, not a browseable region:** WebAIM notes ARIA labels/descriptions are read as a stream (no word-by-word exploration) and semantics are stripped from referenced content, so citation descriptions must be kept succinct and tested in a screen reader ([WebAIM, Introduction to ARIA](https://webaim.org/techniques/aria)).
- **A shipping annotation product claims conformance:** Hypothesis states it is "regularly reviewed by the Inclusive Design Research Centre", conforms to WCAG 2.1 Level AA and Section 508, and documents this in a VPAT ([Hypothesis, Getting Started with Blackboard](https://web.hypothes.is/getting-started-with-blackboard)).
- **Footer-style endnote navigation is documented as burdensome in print/reference terms too:** with large numbers of citations, "finding a particular note can be burdensome", alleviated but not eliminated by hyperlinking ([Western Oregon University Chicago Style Guide](https://research.wou.edu/c.php?g=551307&p=3785495)); e-book conversions that turn footnotes into endnotes are widely reported as making notes less likely to be read ([ACRLog, "Finding Footnotes and Chasing Citations"](https://acrlog.org/2011/10/11/finding-footnotes-and-chasing-citations)).

## Implications for the sources-panel and reader design

Decision-relevant facts only; the choices themselves belong to #12 and #27.

1. **Placement of sources changes verification behaviour over time, not just at first sight.** In a 372-participant controlled study, end-of-footer source lists lost citation use sharply after the fourth message while hover cards and an aligned sidebar sustained or grew it; the aligned sidebar was the only condition where critical-thinking/synthesis scores stayed high as citation density increased ([arXiv:2601.14611](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.14611)).
2. **Readers skim sources: hover/preview interactions dominate, and full-text verification stays under one open per round regardless of interface.** Any provenance design is competing for a very small verification budget ([arXiv:2601.14611](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.14611)).
3. **Two-stage citation access (peek the quote, click to jump to the exact passage) is now the shipped standard in the closest product analogue** — NotebookLM does hover→quote, select→navigate-to-location, and its sources panel adds per-source include/exclude checkboxes plus auto-labelling at 5+ sources ([Google NotebookLM Help](https://support.google.com/notebooklm/answer/16179559?hl=en), [same](https://support.google.com/notebooklm/answer/16206563?hl=en)).
4. **Per-claim attribution to different works is already a mature pattern with a known accuracy caveat:** scite classifies each citation statement as supporting/contrasting/mentioning plus its section, but independent evaluation found human assessors relabelled a large minority of statements ([scite docs](https://docs.scite.ai/guides/smart-citations); [accuracy evaluation](https://scite.ai/reports/scite-a-smart-citation-index-1ZNdYEAM)).
5. **Provenance-deferred (Wikipedia-style "Notes and references" appendix) and provenance-inline (hover cards, sidebars) coexist in every major reference product; the measured difference is friction and density handling, not correctness** — Wikipedia keeps one terminal notes section, hover-previews it, and A/B-tested previews to fewer pageviews but more distinct pages explored ([MOS:Layout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style/Layout); [Page Previews](https://www.mediawiki.org/wiki/Page_Previews); [arXiv:1903.10670](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1903.10670)).
6. **Cross-book comparison has a classical method (Adler: collect passages → neutral terms → neutral propositions → issues) and an empirical dividend:** one-week retention was better after reading multiple texts than after re-reading one ([How to Read a Book, archive.org](https://ia902804.us.archive.org/19/items/howtoreadabook1972edition/How%20to%20Read%20a%20Book%20-%201972%20Edition.pdf); [Instructional Science 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11251-024-09686-4)).
7. **"Also also discussed in…" navigation needs editorial limits to stay useful:** Wikipedia's own guideline requires See also lists to be short, deduplicated against body links, and annotated when relevance isn't obvious; related-work graphs (co-citation similarity) are an alternative that "is not a citation tree" ([MOS:Layout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style/Layout); [Connected Papers About](https://www.connectedpapers.com/about)).
8. **Mobile cannot use hover, and the documented mobile citation pattern is a bottom sheet capped at 50% of the viewport with the page scrolled so the marker stays visible; side-by-side marginalia is width-gated in shipping products** ([Reference Tooltips tablet caveat](https://www.mediawiki.org/wiki/Reference_Tooltips/en); [Mobile design/Reference reveal](https://www.mediawiki.org/wiki/Mobile_design/Reference_reveal); [Readwise blog](https://blog.readwise.io/the-next-chapter-of-reader-public-beta)).
9. **Four WCAG 2.2 AA criteria bind citation-heavy layouts directly:** 1.4.13 (hover cards dismissible/hoverable/persistent and keyboard-reachable), 2.5.8 (24×24 px chip targets), 1.4.10 (any two-pane layout must reflow at 320 px), 2.4.11 (overlays must not fully obscure focused elements) ([W3C Understanding 1.4.13](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/content-on-hover-or-focus); [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22)).
10. **Anchor durability is a fact of life for passage-level provenance:** Hypothesis's documented response to un-re-findable anchors is a visible "orphans" tab with struck-through anchor text rather than deletion — citations to book passages will face the same problem across editions and re-uploads ([Hypothesis orphans](https://web.hypothes.is/blog/showing-orphaned-annotations)).
