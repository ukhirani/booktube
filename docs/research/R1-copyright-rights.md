## 1. Legal treatment of user-uploaded book PDFs

### 1.1 Uploading/scanning a book creates a copy; owning a copy does not authorize making one

- US copyright owners hold the exclusive **reproduction right** (17 U.S.C. §106(1)), the **derivative-work right** (§106(2)) and the **distribution right** (§106(3)) ([Title 17 Ch. 1](https://www.copyright.gov/title17/92chap1.html)).
- The **first-sale doctrine** (§109(a)) only limits the *distribution* right for a lawfully made copy; it does not authorize *reproduction*. In *Capitol Records v. ReDigi* (2d Cir. 2018, Leval, J.) a platform that copied lawfully purchased digital files in order to resell them infringed §106(1), and the copying was held **not** fair use ([Copyright Office fair-use summary PDF](https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/summaries/capitol-records-llc-redigi-inc-no.16-2321-2nd-cir.dec.12.2018.pdf); opinion via [Justia](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca2/16-2321/16-2321-2018-12-12.html)).
- The US has **no general private-copying exception**. *Sony v. Universal* (1984) shielded only narrow noncommercial personal use (time-shifting of TV) ([official U.S. Reports PDF](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-464/pdf/USREPORTS-464-417.pdf)). "I own the book" is therefore not, by itself, a US statutory basis for creating and uploading a digital copy.
- *Hachette Book Group v. Internet Archive* (2d Cir. 2024; cert. denied Dec. 2024) held that **scanning entire print books and serving full digital copies** — even on a one-owned-to-one-loaned basis and even non-commercially — is not fair use; converting entire works into a new format without permission is not transformative and substitutes for licensed editions ([Copyright Office fair-use summary PDF](https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/summaries/Hachette%20Book%20Grp.%20Inc%20v.%20Internet%20Archive%2C%20115%20F.%204th%20163%20%282d%20Cir.%202024%29.pdf); [AAP statement quoting the court](https://publishers.org/news/appeals-court-affirms-decision-against-internet-archive-for-copyright-infringement)).
- Conversely, **transformative internal processing** of copies has been held fair use: *Authors Guild v. Google* (2d Cir. 2015) upheld full-book digitization for full-text search with tightly limited snippet display, and distribution of copies to libraries on conditions of lawful use ([Copyright Office fair-use summary PDF](https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/summaries/authorsguild-google-2dcir2015.pdf); [govinfo opinion PDF](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-ca2-13-04829/pdf/USCOURTS-ca2-13-04829-0.pdf)). Fact: what is protected is the *new function* (search), not re-serving the text.

### 1.2 Storage, retention, deletion and DMCA/takedown exposure (US)

- A platform hosting user uploads can limit its exposure through the **§512(c) safe harbor**, which is conditioned on: registering a **designated DMCA agent** with the Copyright Office and on the site; **expeditious removal** on receipt of a compliant notice; the **counter-notice** procedure (repost not less than 10 and not more than 14 business days after a counter-notice unless the complainant sues); knowledge/"red flag" standards; and a **reasonably implemented repeat-infringer termination policy** (§512(i)). Senders of notices/counter-notices face liability for knowing misrepresentations (§512(f)) ([Copyright Office §512 resources](https://www.copyright.gov/512/index.html); [17 U.S.C. §512](https://www.copyright.gov/title17/92chap5.html)).
- *Lenz v. Universal* (9th Cir. 2015): rightsholders **must consider fair use** before sending a takedown notice; failure raises a triable §512(f) misrepresentation claim ([9th Cir. opinion PDF](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2015/09/14/13-16106.pdf)). Practical fact: takedown and counter-notice traffic will include fair-use disputes, not only clear infringement.
- **Retention is its own liability question.** In *Bartz v. Anthropic* (N.D. Cal., Alsup, J., June 2025) training LLMs on books was fair use, but *keeping* millions of **pirated** books in a permanent internal library was not — all four factors weighed against fair use for those copies ([Copyright Office fair-use summary PDF](https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/summaries/Bartz-v-Anthropic-PBC-787-F-Supp-3d-1007-ND-Cal-2025.pdf); [Loeb case summary](https://www.loeb.com/en/insights/publications/2025/07/bartz-v-anthropic-pbc)). The resulting **$1.5B class settlement received final approval on 2026-07-20** (~482,000 books, roughly $3,000 per book; >91% of claims filed) ([settlement administrator FAQ](https://anthropiccopyrightsettlement.com/faq); [AP report](https://apnews.com/article/ai-anthropic-copyright-settlement-claude-books-bartz-74b140444023898aeba8579b6e9f0d63)).
- Statutory damages exposure per infringed work: up to **$30,000**, and up to **$150,000 if willful** ([17 U.S.C. §504](https://www.copyright.gov/title17/92chap5.html)).
- **No statutory retention period exists for uploaded copies**; copyright law instead makes each retained copy a reproduction that must rest on an authorization/exception, and §512 protection evaporates once the provider has the requisite knowledge and does not act. EU note: if personal data is processed, GDPR storage-limitation applies separately from copyright ([GDPR Art. 5](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj)).

### 1.3 EU platform regime for user uploads (if EU users are served)

- Under **Art. 17 of Directive (EU) 2019/790 (DSM)**, giving the public access to copyright-protected works **uploaded by users** is itself an act of communication to the public: the platform must obtain a licence, or else show (a) best efforts to obtain one, (b) best efforts to ensure unavailability of specific works for which rightsholders gave notice, and (c) expeditious removal plus best efforts to prevent future uploads; providers exceeding **5 million monthly unique visitors** must additionally prevent re-uploads of notified works ([directive text, EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML?uri=CELEX%3A32019L0790)).
- Art. 17(7) also **guarantees users the ability to rely on quotation, criticism, review, parody/caricature/pastiche** exceptions — filters/stay-down must not block those uses ([Commission guidance on Art. 17, 2021](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML?uri=CELEX%3A52021DC0288)).
- EU has no general "AI exception": the TDM exceptions (DSM Arts. 3–4) cover *training* only (Art. 4 subject to machine-readable opt-out), not publication of AI-derived outputs.

## 2. Whether and how AI-generated summaries/transformations of books may be published

- Publishing a summary/abridgement/translation implicates the **derivative-work right** (§106(2)); the only defenses are permission/licence or **fair use**, which is judged case-by-case with no categorical rule. *Campbell v. Acuff-Rose* (1994): commercial purpose alone does not create a presumption against fair use; factors are weighed together, case by case ([Copyright Office fair-use summary PDF](https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/summaries/campbell-acuff-1994.pdf); [official opinion PDF](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-510/pdf/USREPORTS-510-569.pdf)).
- **Close summaries lose.** *Nihon Keizai Shimbun v. Comline* (2d Cir. 1999): a competitor's "abstracts" of news articles were **not** fair use — they were "not in the least transformative", tracked the originals in structure and content, and superseded them in the market ([Copyright Office fair-use summary PDF](https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/summaries/nihonkeizai-comline-2ndcir1999.pdf)). Market substitution is the decisive concern: *Harper & Row v. Nation* (1985) called market effect "the single most important element of fair use" ([official opinion PDF](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-471/pdf/USREPORTS-471-539.pdf)).
- **Training ≠ publishing output — and output risk is live.** *Kadrey v. Meta* (N.D. Cal. June 2025) granted Meta fair use on that record while flagging "market dilution" as a potentially cognizable harm that plaintiffs had not adequately evidenced ([court opinion PDF](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.cand.415175/gov.uscourts.cand.415175.598.0_1.pdf); [Copyright Office summary](https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/summaries/Kadrey-v-Meta-Platforms-Inc-788-F-Supp-3d-1028-ND-Cal-2025.pdf)). *Thomson Reuters v. Ross* (D. Del. Feb./May 2025) denied fair use for a non-generative AI built on copied headnotes; certified for interlocutory appeal (3d Cir. No. 25-2153, argued 2025) ([district court summary PDF via UCI mirror](https://bpb-us-e2.wpmucdn.com/sites.uci.edu/dist/d/2220/files/2025/07/Thomson-Reuters-Enterprise-Centre-GmbH-v-Ross-Intelligence-Inc_Redacted.pdf); [3d Cir. briefing](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.ca3.125346/gov.uscourts.ca3.125346.40.0.pdf)).
- **NYT v. OpenAI/Microsoft (S.D.N.Y., No. 1:25-md-03143)** remains undecided: the Third Amended Complaint (Aug. 2026) alleges GenAI outputs "recite Times content verbatim, closely summarize it, and mimic its expressive style" ([complaint PDF on CourtListener](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.nysd.640396/gov.uscourts.nysd.640396.1677.0.pdf)); cross-motions for summary judgment on fair use were filed 2026-09-04 and are pending before Judge Stein ([report](https://www.mercurynews.com/2026/09/04/daily-news-ny-times-want-fair-use-claim-tossed-in-copyright-case-against-openai-microsoft-2/)).
- **EU output liability already decided once:** *GEMA v. OpenAI*, Munich I Regional Court, 11 Nov. 2025 (No. 42 O 14139/24): memorization of lyrics inside the model and faithful reproduction in outputs were held **reproductions/communication to the public**; "implied consent by online publication" and the TDM exception were rejected; the **model provider, not only the user, was liable**; injunction + damages, fines up to €250,000; first instance, under appeal ([EUIPO case note](https://www.euipo.europa.eu/en/law/recent-case-law/the-higher-regional-court-of-munich-considered-memorization-and-temporary-copies-occurred-in-model-training-as-infringing-reproductions-of-works)).
- **AI outputs are largely uncopyrightable.** US Copyright Office, *Copyright and Artificial Intelligence, Part 2: Copyrightability* (Jan. 2025): AI-generated material is protected **only** where a human determined sufficient expressive elements; prompting alone is not authorship ([report PDF](https://www.copyright.gov/ai/Copyright-and-Artificial-Intelligence-Part-2-Copyrightability-Report.pdf)). *Part 3: Generative AI Training* (pre-pub, May 2025): training implicates the reproduction right; fair use is case-by-case; market dilution and lost-licensing harms count; outputs substantially similar to inputs make a "strong argument" that model weights themselves infringe ([report PDF](https://www.copyright.gov/ai/Copyright-and-Artificial-Intelligence-Part-3-Generative-AI-Training-Report-Pre-Publication-Version.pdf); status page [copyright.gov/ai](https://www.copyright.gov/ai/)).
- **Platform precedent on publishing summaries:** Amazon KDP content guidelines state that "companion books based on copyrighted works (e.g., summaries, study guides, etc.)" **may not be published outside the U.S. without written permission from the copyright holder**, and require disclosure of AI-generated content at submission ([KDP Content Guidelines](https://kdp.amazon.com/help/topic/G200672390)).

## 3. Quote length, excerpt and attribution norms

- **US: there is no statutory word-count, sentence-count or percentage limit.** §107 lists illustrative purposes and four factors ([17 U.S.C. §107](https://www.copyright.gov/title17/92chap1.html)). Quantity is not decisive: *Harper & Row* found infringement from taking a few hundred words — the "heart" — of a ~200,000-word manuscript, while *Campbell* rejected mechanical limits in favor of case-by-case weighing ([Harper & Row opinion PDF](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-471/pdf/USREPORTS-471-539.pdf); [Campbell opinion PDF](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-510/pdf/USREPORTS-510-569.pdf)).
- **UK: fair dealing with a fixed acknowledgement requirement, still no numeric limit.** CDPA 1988 s.30: fair dealing for criticism/review/quotation is allowed only if the work was lawfully made available to the public, the dealing is **fair** (quotation "no more than is required by the specific purpose"), and it carries a **sufficient acknowledgement** (title + author) ([legislation.gov.uk s.30](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1988/48/section/30)). Government guidance: "no hard and fast rules"; a short necessary quotation may be fair, "it would not permit a long extract"; acknowledgement "generally means the title and the author's name" ([GOV.UK exceptions to copyright](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/exceptions-to-copyright)).
- **EU:** the InfoSoc quotation exception (Art. 5(3)(d)) and Art. 17(7)(a) DSM preserve quotation/criticism/review on platforms; quotation requires attribution under national implementations.
- **US attribution:** credit is *not* an element of fair use, but **17 U.S.C. §1202(b)** separately forbids intentionally removing/altering copyright management information (title, author, copyright owner) knowing it will "induce, enable, facilitate, or conceal" infringement ([17 U.S.C. §1202](https://www.copyright.gov/title17/92chap12.html)). §1202 claims are now standard in AI litigation — *Kadrey* dismissed its §1202 claim only because the underlying copying was held fair use ([§1202 order PDF](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.cand.415175/gov.uscourts.cand.415175.601.0.pdf)). Fact: pipelines that strip source attribution create a **separate** statutory claim even where copying might be fair.

## 4. Rights required for public distribution of content derived from a privately uploaded book

- A private upload grants the platform/user **no publication rights**. Publicly distributing a summary, translation, excerpt collection or narration built from the book exercises §106(1)–(3) rights in the original unless licensed or fair use.
- Commercial scale increases the risk: the summary/abridgement market is one rightsholders license — see *Nihon Keizai* (§2) and publisher licensing markets validated in *Hachette* (§1.1).
- Platform-level authorisation regimes by geography: **US** — host user posts under §512 (§1.2); **EU** — Art. 17 licence-or-diligence regime (§1.3); **UK** — no Art. 17 equivalent, but host liability and fair-dealing limits apply as above.
- Access ≠ consent: the Munich court in *GEMA v. OpenAI* held that publishing works online covers only "uses that may be expected and are usual" — memorisation/regurgitation was not ([Library of Congress Global Legal Monitor summary](https://www.loc.gov/item/global-legal-monitor/2026-01-13/germany-court-prohibits-memorization-and-reproduction-of-copyrighted-song-lyrics-in-ai-models)).
- Contract allocation used across the industry: the user warrants rights and indemnifies the platform (see §7).

## 5. AI narration of copyrighted book text (full audiobook vs summaries)

- **Full-book narration = reproducing the whole text in a new recording.** Recording and distributing an audiobook exercises the reproduction and derivative rights; audio rights are **licensed separately** from print/ebook rights. In ACX (Audible) agreements the uploading party must warrant that it **owns or controls distribution rights to the Audiobook**, "has obtained all rights necessary", that use will not infringe third-party rights, and must indemnify ([Audiobook License and Distribution Agreement](https://help.acx.com/s/article/audiobook-license-and-distribution-agreement-ua); [ACX Book Posting Agreement](https://help.acx.com/s/article/acx-book-posting-agreement-ua)).
- **Industry distribution gate on AI narration:** ACX submission requirements state audiobooks "must be narrated by a human unless otherwise authorized" and that "unauthorized use of text-to-speech, AI, or automated recordings in ACX titles is prohibited" ([ACX audio submission requirements](https://help.acx.com/s/article/what-are-the-acx-audio-submission-requirements)).
- **Narrated summaries** face the same analysis as written summaries (amount taken, purpose, market substitution — §2); narration adds no independent exception.
- **Voice/likeness layer (separate from copyright):** e.g. Tennessee's ELVIS Act (effective 2024-07-01) creates a property right in an individual's **voice**, expressly including simulation, and liability for distributing tools whose primary purpose is producing an unauthorized voice ([bill text](https://www.capitol.tn.gov/Bills/113/Bill/SB2096.pdf); [Governor's signing release](https://www.tn.gov/governor/news/2024/3/21/photos--gov--lee-signs-elvis-act-into-law/)). Generic synthetic voices raise no third-party voice right but inherit the text-rights issues above.

## 6. Commercial use and creator monetization of derived content

- Commerciality is a **factor, not a bar**: *Campbell* removed any presumption against commercial fair use, but *Harper & Row*/*Hachette* show commercial substitution loses ([Campbell PDF](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-510/pdf/USREPORTS-510-569.pdf)).
- **Platform monetization rules are stricter than copyright and apply independently:**
  - **YouTube Partner Program:** monetization requires "original and authentic" content. "Reused content" — repurposing content from other sources **without adding significant original commentary, substantive modifications or educational/entertainment value** — is ineligible for monetization, and the policy applies "even if you have permission from the original creator"; it is explicitly separate from copyright/fair use. From 2025-07-15 the renamed **"inauthentic content"** rule targets repetitive/mass-produced content ([YouTube Channel Monetisation Policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)).
  - **YouTube AI disclosure:** creators must disclose realistic **altered or synthetic content**; labels are attached to descriptions (and on-video for sensitive topics) ([YouTube Blog, 2024-03-18](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/disclosing-ai-generated-content/)). YouTube has stated AI content remains monetizable if it adds creative value and is disclosed ([YPP Q&A](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)).
  - **Amazon KDP:** AI-**generated** text/images/translations must be disclosed at publish time (AI-**assisted** editing need not be); the publisher "is responsible for verifying that all AI-generated and/or AI-assisted content … [complies] with all applicable intellectual property rights"; companion summaries/study guides of copyrighted works are restricted outside the US ([KDP Content Guidelines](https://kdp.amazon.com/help/topic/G200672390)).
  - **ACX/Audible:** royalties flow only to parties who control audio rights (royalty-share deals split ~20–25% to rights holder/producer) ([ACX royalty terms](https://help.acx.com/s/article/acx-royalty-payment-terms-and-procedures-for-rights-holders-and-producers-royalty-share-deals-audible-exclusive-distribution-rights-ua)).
- **Comparable summary businesses operate on permissions:** getAbstract's co-founder states publicly that it "works with more than 600 publishers to secure the rights to summarize their content" and only summarizes titles it has the right to ([first-party statement](https://www.quora.com/How-do-companies-like-Blinkist-not-violate-copyright-by-posting-book-summaries)); Blinkist describes human-produced Blinks and says its writers/editors "collaborate with the original authors whenever possible" ([Blinkist FAQ](https://www.blinkist.com/)). No public source shows a large summary service relying on unlicensed wholesale use.

## 7. How comparable platforms handle copyright complaints and what they contractually require

**Statutory baseline (US service providers):** designated agent + public policy page; compliant notice handling; counter-notice with 10–14 business-day repost window; documented and reasonably implemented **repeat-infringer termination** policy; no red-flag knowledge ([Copyright Office §512 FAQ](https://www.copyright.gov/512/index.html)).

| Platform | User warranty / contractual requirement | Complaint handling | Termination |
|---|---|---|---|
| **YouTube/Google** | Terms require original content and compliance with copyright policies; separate Content ID claim layer for rightsholders | Copyright strikes: content removed; counter-notification or retraction resolves; **3 active strikes in 90 days → channel termination** ([copyright strikes](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2814000)) | Google-wide repeat-infringer policy: warnings/restrictions after one complaint; "in appropriate circumstances, we terminate the accounts of repeat infringers" ([Google legal](https://support.google.com/legal/answer/11625144)) |
| **Wattpad** | ToS: "Don't submit content you don't hold the copyright for (unless you have permission…)"; adaptations/translations require the copyright owner's explicit permission; "giving credit … or slightly adapting" does not cure | DMCA policy applied globally; removes content alleged infringing **without prior notice** at its sole discretion; designated agent contact in ToS ([Wattpad ToS](https://wattpad.com/terms)) | "In appropriate circumstances, we will also terminate a user's account if the user is determined to be a repeat infringer" (same) |
| **Amazon KDP** | Publisher warrants it has the rights needed; responsible for IP compliance; AI-generated content disclosure required; companion summaries restricted outside US | Content removed for infringement; report-violations workflow ([KDP guidelines](https://kdp.amazon.com/help/topic/G200672390)) | Account termination under Amazon policies for violating rights |
| **ACX/Audible** | Warrants ownership/control of audiobook distribution rights + "all rights necessary" + indemnity; human narration rule ([ACX agreement](https://help.acx.com/s/article/audiobook-license-and-distribution-agreement-ua)) | Rights verification documents may be demanded before distribution; infringing submissions rejected ([ACX posting agreement](https://help.acx.com/s/article/acx-book-posting-agreement-ua)) | Contract termination + payment withholding |

**Recurring contract pattern across all four:** (1) user warrants ownership/permission; (2) indemnification of the platform; (3) published DMCA agent + takedown/counter-notice flow; (4) discretionary removal without notice; (5) repeat-infringer termination; (6) AI-content disclosure/labeling; (7) separate monetization/originality rules that do not depend on copyright.

## 8. Cross-jurisdiction snapshot

- **US:** flexible four-factor fair use; §512 safe-harbour package; no private-copy exception; AI outputs uncopyrightable absent human authorship (CO Part 2); AI training/output law unsettled (NYT v OpenAI SJ pending as of 2026-09).
- **EU:** Art. 17 licence-or-diligence for user uploads; TDM exceptions for training only (with opt-out); no general AI exception; first-instance liability found for model memorisation/regurgitation (*GEMA*).
- **UK:** fair dealing for criticism/review/quotation only, always with sufficient acknowledgement; no numeric quote limit; amount must be no more than the purpose requires.
- Comparable platforms already harmonise on one operational baseline (agent, notices, counter-notices, repeat-infringer termination, warranties, AI disclosure) regardless of jurisdiction.

## Implications for the product-constraints decision

Facts/constraints a product decision will have to be built on (facts only; choices belong to the human decision ticket):

1. **Server-side copies of uploaded books have no blanket US legal cover:** the US has no general personal-use copying exception, and first sale/ownership does not authorize reproduction (*ReDigi*, *Hachette*). Any "users upload PDFs of books they own" design rests on user authorization + liability allocation, not on a private-copy right. ([ReDigi summary](https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/summaries/capitol-records-llc-redigi-inc-no.16-2321-2nd-cir.dec.12.2018.pdf), [Hachette summary](https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/summaries/Hachette%20Book%20Grp.%20Inc%20v.%20Internet%20Archive%2C%20115%20F.%204th%20163%20%282d%20Cir.%202024%29.pdf))
2. **Hosting user content publicly in the US is only safe-harbour-defensible with the full §512 package:** Copyright Office-registered agent, expeditious notice-and-takedown, statutory counter-notice/repost window, and a documented repeat-infringer termination policy. ([Copyright Office §512](https://www.copyright.gov/512/index.html))
3. **EU users trigger the Art. 17 licence-or-diligence regime** (licence or best-efforts licensing + notice-and-stay-down + re-upload prevention over 5M MAU), with mandatory headroom for quotation/criticism/review/parody — a materially different compliance surface from the US. ([Directive 2019/790](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML?uri=CELEX%3A32019L0790))
4. **Retention of unlicensed copies is independently actionable:** *Bartz* treated permanent retention of unlicensed books as infringement even though training was fair use; the $1.5B settlement (~$3,000/book, final approval 2026-07-20) shows per-work exposure. ([Bartz summary](https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/summaries/Bartz-v-Anthropic-PBC-787-F-Supp-3d-1007-ND-Cal-2025.pdf), [settlement FAQ](https://anthropiccopyrightsettlement.com/faq))
5. **Published AI summaries are judged on closeness to the original and market substitution, not on being "AI":** competitor abstracts that tracked originals lost fair use (*Nihon Keizai*); Amazon KDP already gates companion summaries/study guides of copyrighted works behind written permission outside the US. ([Comline summary](https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/summaries/nihonkeizai-comline-2ndcir1999.pdf), [KDP guidelines](https://kdp.amazon.com/help/topic/G200672390))
6. **AI-generated summaries are likely not copyrightable themselves** (CO Part 2: human authorship required; prompting insufficient) — no copyright moat over outputs, while **regurgitation of source text is actionable** (Munich *GEMA* order against the model provider; NYT allegations pending). ([Part 2 PDF](https://www.copyright.gov/ai/Copyright-and-Artificial-Intelligence-Part-2-Copyrightability-Report.pdf), [EUIPO *GEMA* note](https://www.euipo.europa.eu/en/law/recent-case-law/the-higher-regional-court-of-munich-considered-memorization-and-temporary-copies-occurred-in-model-training-as-infringing-reproductions-of-works))
7. **Quote/excerpt rules have no safe word count in the US** (qualitative taking — the "heart" — can defeat a numerically small quote), while UK/EU quotation requires attribution and "no more than required by purpose"; separately, stripping author/title metadata risks a §1202 CMI claim. ([Harper & Row PDF](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-471/pdf/USREPORTS-471-539.pdf), [GOV.UK guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/exceptions-to-copyright), [§1202](https://www.copyright.gov/title17/92chap12.html))
8. **Full-text AI audiobooks require audio rights, and the dominant audiobook distributor contractually prohibits unauthorized AI narration** (ACX: human-narrated "unless otherwise authorized"); narrated summaries inherit the text-summary analysis. ([ACX requirements](https://help.acx.com/s/article/what-are-the-acx-audio-submission-requirements))
9. **Creator monetization has a second, non-copyright gate:** YouTube demonetizes "reused content" without significant original commentary *even when permission exists*, bars "inauthentic"/mass-produced content (since 2025-07-15), and requires disclosure of realistic altered/synthetic content — facts that constrain monetization design independently of infringement risk. ([YPP policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392))
10. **The industry-standard contract pattern is fixed and observable:** user rights warranty + indemnity + registered DMCA agent + notice/counter-notice flow + repeat-infringer termination + discretionary no-notice removal + AI-content disclosure (Wattpad, KDP, ACX, YouTube; see §7) — these are the terms comparable platforms require of users/creators before hosting or paying out.
