# R2 — Comparable products and anti-doomscrolling precedents

Wayfinder RESEARCH ticket: #4 — "Comparable products and anti-doomscrolling precedents".
Scope: survey of book-summary/insight products, social reading products, and documented
anti-doomscrolling precedents (intent-based feeds, session boundaries, explainable
recommendations, usage controls / wellbeing modes, scroll and attention reminders,
deliberate completion states, feed reset and tuning), plus what demonstrably worked,
what backfired, and which patterns are evidence-backed.

**This document surfaces facts only. It does not propose BookTube's anti-doomscrolling
contract — that decision belongs to a separate human ticket (#8).**

**Evidence labels used throughout:**

- **[Documented]** — verifiable in primary sources (official docs, product pages, engineering blogs, legislation). Describes what a product actually ships or claims it ships.
- **[Peer-reviewed]** — experimental / causal evidence from published research.
- **[Claimed]** — vendor-reported metric or marketing statement, no independent verification found.
- **[Contested]** — credible sources disagree, or the result is disputed / methodologically challenged.

## 1. Book-summary and insight products

### 1.1 Blinkist

- **Format is inherently finite:** a Blink condenses a book's insights into 7–8 "key ideas" of ~200–300 words each, readable or audible in ~15 minutes ([Blinkist Help Center](https://support.blinkist.com/en/articles/10033202-what-is-a-blink)). The unit of consumption is a bounded object, not a feed.
- **Curation is human, and marketed as anti-algorithm:** Blinkist's "How it works" page opens with "Great books. Smart people. No algorithms." — selection comes from bestseller lists, user suggestions and expert input, distilled and proofread by human writers ([Blinkist](https://www.blinkist.com/en/making_a_blink)). **[Claimed]** as positioning; Blinkist simultaneously markets "personalized daily recommendations" and an AI layer that turns videos/podcasts/documents into summaries on the Pro plan ([Blinkist home](https://www.blinkist.com/)). The tension between "no algorithms" marketing and shipped personalization is a useful illustration: products in this category sell *curation + boundedness*, not feeds.
- Library scale ~9,000 titles; positioned around filling "little blocks of unallocated time" (commuting, chores) rather than leisure scrolling ([About Blinkist](https://www.blinkist.com/en/about)).

### 1.2 Shortform

- Structure per book: a **1-Page Summary**, then a **Full Guide** broken into chapters, plus **interactive exercises** whose answers become public discussion posts ([Shortform usage guide](https://www.shortform.com/blog/how-to-use-shortform/)).
- Explicit depth-over-breadth positioning: "You'll get more out of understanding your favorite 50 books deeply than you would by glancing over 5,000 mediocre books" ([About Shortform](https://www.shortform.com/about)).
- Differentiates on density, not time-to-consume: its own comparison table claims 62 ideas/insights for *How to Win Friends…* vs 11 and 9 for unnamed competitors ([Shortform Summaries](https://www.shortform.com/summaries)). **[Claimed]** (vendor comparison).
- Notable for BookTube's "bite-sized article contract": Shortform's design intent is retention and application (exercises, actionables), i.e. a **task/completion orientation** rather than an engagement orientation.

### 1.3 getAbstract

- Every summary is a fixed 15-minute object with a standard skeleton: Rating → Recommendation ("why the content is relevant to you") → Take-Aways → Summary (≤10 min) → Quotes → About the Author → **Source Link to the original work** ([How getAbstract summaries work](https://www.getabstract.com/en/how-it-works)). The always-present source link is an attribution/grounding pattern worth noting for AI-derived summaries.
- Human-authored: "all our summaries are still written by human experts"; AI is "helpful as an assistant in the editing process" ([getAbstract benefits](https://www.getabstract.com/en/benefits)). **[Documented]** as company policy, not audited.
- Library ~50k "knowledge pieces", 8 languages, enterprise/LMS-oriented ([Knowledge library](https://www.getabstract.com/en/knowledge-library)).

### 1.4 Readwise — the strongest "finite session" precedent in this category

- The core loop is a **Daily Review**: a bounded, resurfaced batch of your own highlights, explicitly built on spaced repetition and active recall ([Readwise docs](https://docs.readwise.io/readwise)). Users report Daily Review habits "almost 1,000 days straight" (anecdotal, vendor-reported) ([Readwise blog](https://blog.readwise.io/adding-intention-to-spaced-repetition/)). **[Claimed]**
- **The resurfacing algorithm is user-tunable.** Each document's chance of appearing in today's review is proportional to its share of your highlights (e.g. 100 of 500 highlights → 20% chance), and users can tune each document up or down ("Frequency Tuning") ([Readwise FAQ](https://docs.readwise.io/readwise/docs/faqs/reviewing-highlights)). The company describes it in exactly feed-like terms: "Tuning is like setting up a custom algorithmic feed for your own material which can be tweaked over and over as your interests change" ([Readwise blog](https://blog.readwise.io/hack-your-brain-with-spaced-repetition-and-active-recall/)).
- **Review is split into two explicit halves**: unprocessed new highlights, then Mastery cards on a recall-probability half-life schedule (7/14/28 days for soon/later/someday) — i.e. a *declared, inspectable* scheduling policy rather than an opaque ranker ([Readwise FAQ](https://docs.readwise.io/readwise/docs/faqs/reviewing-highlights)).
- Themed Reviews let users bound a review to a subset (tags, books, an "Inbox"), giving session scoping ([Readwise blog](https://blog.readwise.io/adding-intention-to-spaced-repetition/)).

### 1.5 Audible and Storytel (audio)

- **Audible: clips, notes, bookmarks.** Users cut 5–44 second audio clips, attach text notes, and bookmark positions; clips are app-only ([Audible Help: Manage clips & notes](https://help.audible.com/s/article/manage-clips-bookmarks?language=en_US)). **Whispersync for Voice** syncs reading/listening position across Kindle and Audible, including synchronized text highlighting ([Audible Help: Whispersync](https://help.audible.co.uk/s/article/listen-with-whispersync-for-voice?language=en_GB)). These are *extraction and resumption* affordances: capture a passage, return to a position.
- **Storytel: AI Recaps.** "Recaps" generate a concise summary of previous plot points so users can resume after a break; "Sleep Timer Recaps" summarize the section played while a sleep timer ran. Storytel reports internal data that 100k+ users in Sweden use the sleep timer nightly and 63% struggle to find where they drifted off (**[Claimed]**, internal metrics) ([Storytel Group announcement](https://www.storytelgroup.com/en/storytels-new-ai-powered-recaps-let-users-pick-up-where-they-left-off/)). The stated product goal is *finishing more books*, not more time-in-app.
- **Search in Book** (search inside e-books *and* audiobooks via format-mapping) and **Synced Listening** (read-along text) are re-orientation affordances for long-form content ([Storytel app features](https://www.storytel.com/tv/c/app-features)).

### 1.6 Cross-cutting observations (book-summary category)

1. **No product in this category uses an engagement-optimized infinite feed as its core surface.** Consumption units are bounded (15-minute summary, one Daily Review, one book, one clip). Discovery exists (Blinkist recommendations, getAbstract ratings) but sits *around* the bounded unit. **[Documented]**
2. **Retention mechanics here are spaced-repetition, not scrollers** — the "come back tomorrow" hook is a finite queue that empties (Readwise Daily Review; Duolingo-style daily goal elsewhere).
3. **Marketing vs evidence:** "No algorithms" (Blinkist) and "personalized recommendations" appear in the same product family; treat category marketing copy as positioning, not architecture. **[Claimed]**
4. **No peer-reviewed evidence was found that reading summaries produces durable learning equivalent to reading books.** Absence of evidence noted, not evidence of absence.

## 2. Social reading products

### 2.1 Goodreads

- Recommendation engine launched 2011 as "the Netflix of book recommendations": combines proprietary algorithms over co-shelving and co-enjoyment graphs ("20 billion data points" **[Claimed]**), requires ~20 rated books to work, and is shelf-based (each user-created shelf can be included/excluded from recs) ([Goodreads blog, 2011](https://www.goodreads.com/blog/show/303-announcing-goodreads-personalized-recommendations)).
- User tuning surface is simple and explicit: rate more books, edit favorite genres, create shelves, and press **"Not interested"** on any recommendation ([Goodreads Help](https://help.goodreads.com/s/article/How-Can-I-Improve-my-Recommendations)).
- Social layer is "see what your friends are reading" + bookshelves + reviews + personalized recs ([About Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/about/us?linkId=321627153)). The feed is friend-activity-based, not a short-video ranker; there is no documented completion state or session boundary.
- Useful contrast: Goodreads' *rating graph* approach means recommendation quality scales with explicit user input (ratings), not with passive watch-time signals.

### 2.2 The StoryGraph — explicit anti-black-box positioning

- Recommendations are driven by a **Reading Preferences Survey** with a free-text box: "you can tell us exactly what you're looking for in a book… Filling out the free-text box… is the key to taking your recommendations from good to 'was this book literally written for me?'" Answers can be edited and the survey retaken at any time ([StoryGraph Recommendations Survey Guidelines](https://app.thestorygraph.com/recommendations/guidelines)). This is **intent declaration, not behavioural inference**, as the primary control.
- Founder's stated rationale (public roadmap comment): "something that came up was a distrust of algorithms and how everything these days is an algorithm telling you what you would like… That's why we mainly went down the: *tell us what you're interested in and have a chance to change that whenever you want* route — taking advantage of an algorithmic power, whilst still giving the user some control." ([StoryGraph roadmap](https://roadmap.thestorygraph.com/requests-ideas/posts/will-i-like-this-book-)). **[Documented]** as design intent.
- The personalized recommendations surface is presented as lists, including an "**Out of your comfort zone**" list, plus advanced search — browse-shaped, not scroll-shaped ([StoryGraph changelog](https://roadmap.thestorygraph.com/changelog/revamped-recommendations)).
- They explicitly declined a single Netflix-style "% fit" score; community preference is for explanations tied to declared mood/pace/genre preferences (same roadmap thread).

### 2.3 Letterboxd (film analogue)

- Core objects are **diary, watchlist, lists, ratings, reviews**; the activity timeline is friends' activity, filterable by activity type for paid users ("reviews only on mobile") — i.e. **user-configured feed composition** ([Letterboxd FAQ](https://letterboxd.com/faq/), [Letterboxd journal](https://letterboxd.com/journal/growing-learning/)).
- **Feed-hygiene precedent:** when members back-fill diary entries older than two weeks, Letterboxd shows followers **a maximum of one item per hour "to prevent this activity from saturating their feeds"** ([Letterboxd Help](https://letterboxd.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/15179259045903-Why-do-my-reviews-sometimes-not-appear-in-Activity)). A deliberate, documented rate-limit against feed flooding — the opposite of engagement-maximizing distribution. **[Documented]**
- **Completion/closure artifacts:** a **Diary** (what I did, when), **Year in Review** (requires ≥10 diary entries in a year; Pro), and an annual email recap ([Letterboxd FAQ](https://letterboxd.com/faq/)). Sessions end with reflection, not with "next".
- Letterboxd is ad-free-ish/Pro-funded and independent; it has no documented infinite recommender feed.

### 2.4 Other analogues noted (brief)

- **BeReal** is the clearest *session-boundary* social product: one post per day, a random-time notification, a two-minute window, and — critically — **you cannot see friends' posts until you have posted your own** ([App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/bereal-photos-friends-daily/id1459645446), [BeReal privacy policy](https://bereal.com/privacy), [WIRED explainer](https://www.wired.com/story/how-to-use-bereal/)). The feed is wiped daily. **[Documented]**
- **Duolingo** (learning, not reading) is the canonical *finite daily goal* product — see §4.1.

## 3. Documented anti-doomscrolling precedents

### 3.1 Deliberate completion states

| Precedent | What it does | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Instagram "You're All Caught Up" (2018) | Marks the end of posts from the last 2 days; older/seen posts appear below it | **[Documented]** — [Instagram announcement](https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/introducing-youre-all-caught-up-in-feed) |
| **…and its erosion (2020)** | Instagram appended **Suggested Posts + ads after the completion marker**, letting users keep scrolling past "caught up" | **[Documented]** — [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/2020/8/19/21373809/instagram-suggested-posts-update-end-feed), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/08/19/instagram-finds-new-ad-space-at-the-end-of-your-feed-with-launch-of-suggested-posts-feature/), Instagram engineering ["Designing a Constrained Exploration System"](https://about.instagram.com/blog/engineering/designing-a-constrained-exploration-system) |
| Instagram's own caveat | "People who follow a larger number of accounts may never reach the end of their feed" — completion states only fire if the followed-content set is small enough | **[Documented]** — TechCrunch (above) |
| Netflix "Are you still watching?" | Pauses autoplay after 3 episodes / 90 min uninterrupted; frames it as not losing your place / not burning data; users can disable via autoplay settings | **[Documented]** — [Netflix Help](https://help.netflix.com/en/node/114059), [Netflix methodology](https://advertising.netflix.com/en-us/article/netflix-description-of-methodology) |
| BeReal daily wipe | Content cleared daily; late posts labelled "15 minutes late"; post-to-unlock | **[Documented]** — links in §2.4 |
| Letterboxd Diary / Year in Review | Session closes into a log and an annual reflection | **[Documented]** — §2.3 |

**Key fact:** the best-known completion state in social media was shipped, then *deliberately monetized into a continuation surface*. Instagram's Robby Stein: "We see people continuing to seek out more posts they're interested in after catching up with their feeds, so we wanted to… make it easier to go a little deeper" (TechCrunch, above). The Verge called it "a major reversal from Instagram's stance two years ago on screen time."

### 3.2 Session boundaries, usage controls and wellbeing modes

- **YouTube "Take a break" reminder** pauses the video after a user-set interval (15/30/60/90/180 min or custom). **Default: ON for ages 13–17, OFF for 18+.** Timer resets on app close, sign-out, device switch, or >30 min pause ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9012523)). Related tools: time-watched profile, bedtime reminder, autoplay toggle ([YouTube wellbeing page](https://www.youtube.com/intl/ALL_in/howyoutubeworks/our-commitments/promoting-digital-wellbeing/)).
- **Instagram "Take a Break"** (Dec 2021): after 10/20/30 minutes of scrolling, a prompt with "expert-backed tips." Meta reports early tests showed >90% of teens who set reminders keep them on **[Claimed]** ([Meta Newsroom](https://about.fb.com/news/2021/12/new-teen-safety-tools-on-instagram/)). Builds on an existing "Daily Limit" tool. Break reminders were later folded into the daily time-limit tool ([Instagram blog update](https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/raising-the-standard-for-protecting-teens-and-supporting-parents-online)).
- **Instagram night-time nudges:** teens spending >10 min in Reels/DMs late at night get a "it's late, close the app" nudge; Facebook teens get a 20-minute prompt; **Quiet Mode** mutes notifications and auto-replies ([Meta Newsroom, June 2023](https://about.fb.com/news/2023/06/parental-supervision-and-teen-time-management-on-metas-apps/)).
- **Instagram Teen Accounts (Sept 2024):** *default-on* 60-minute "leave the app" reminder and **Sleep Mode 10pm–7am** (notifications muted, DM auto-replies, "reminder to close Instagram") ([Instagram announcement](https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/instagram-teen-accounts), [Help Center](https://help.instagram.com/995996839195964/)). **[Documented]** — this is the strongest *default* (not opt-in) boundary among the major apps.
- **TikTok:** every under-18 account gets a **60-minute daily screen-time limit by default**; continuing requires a passcode ("active decision"). If a teen opts out and exceeds 100 min/day, TikTok prompts them to set their own limit; TikTok reported this raised screen-time-tool usage by **234%** **[Claimed]**, plus a weekly screen-time recap notification ([TikTok Newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/new-features-for-teens-and-families-on-tiktok-us), [well-being guide](https://www.tiktok.com/safety/en/well-being-guide)).
- **TikTok "Wind Down" / "Sleep Hours":** under-16s on TikTok after 10pm get a full-screen takeover with calming music; a second, **harder-to-dismiss** prompt follows if they continue; no night-time push notifications to teens, ever. Expanded to a guided-meditation interruption, **on by default for under-18s**. TikTok claims 98% of teens in testing kept the meditation on **[Claimed]** ([TikTok Newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/new-ways-we-are-supporting-parents-and-helping-teens-build-balanced-digital-habits), [TikTok Newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/tiktok-expanding-mental-health-education-fund-and-wellbeing-features), [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/news/668051/tiktok-guided-meditation-wind-down-10pm)).
- **Pinterest school-hours prompt:** under-18 users opening Pinterest during school hours get "come see us after school" plus a link to disable notifications during school hours — company-stated policy from CEO Bill Ready ([TED podcast transcript](https://www.ted.com/podcasts/ted.com/podcasts/fixing-social-media-with-pinterest-ceo-bill-ready-transcript)). **[Claimed]** (executive statement, no independent audit).

### 3.3 Intent-based feeds and non-profiling recommender choice

- **Regulation (binding for very large platforms):** DSA **Article 38** — VLOPs/VLOSEs using recommender systems "shall provide at least one option for each of their recommender systems which is not based on profiling" ([DSA Art. 38 text](https://www.digitalacts.eu/regulation/digital-service-act/article/38/recommender-systems); official: [EUR-Lex Reg. 2022/2065](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2022/2065/oj)). Article 27 adds ranking-transparency duties.
- **EDPB interpretation is design-relevant:** providers "should present options equally and should not nudge users to select the option for a recommender system that is based on profiling. While the non-profiling based option is active, the provider… should not continue to collect and process personal data to profile the user." ([EDPB Guidelines 3/2025, PDF](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2025-09/edpb_guidelines_202503_interplay-dsa-gdpr_v1_en.pdf)). **[Documented]** — i.e. regulators treat *equal presentation of feed modes* and *profiling cessation in non-profiling mode* as requirements, not niceties.
- **Bluesky: algorithmic choice as architecture.** Default timeline is chronological "timelines" (posts from accounts you follow); third parties ship **feed generators** (custom algorithms) that users install/unpin; "we provide an open marketplace of feeds and let users choose which ones they want to use" ([Bluesky blog](https://bsky.social/about/blog/7-27-2023-custom-feeds), [Bluesky docs](https://docs.bsky.app/docs/starter-templates/custom-feeds), [AT Protocol guide](https://atproto.com/guides/feeds)). Feeds have names, descriptions and creators visible in the protocol. **[Documented]**
- **Pinterest: intent/explicit-signal ranking.** Its 10-K describes "the unique, first party, **intent-based signal** we receive from user actions" powering recommendations ([Pinterest 10-K via EDGAR mirror](https://app.edgar.tools/filing/1506293/0001506293-26-000021)). Pinterest publicly argues for ranking on "inspired actions" (saves) rather than views: "when we prioritize what gets 'saved'… the images and videos that are top-performing don't distract you from your life" ([Pinterest business blog](https://business.pinterest.com/blog/a-better-internet-is-possible/)) — **[Claimed]** as outcome, **[Documented]** as stated policy. CEO frames the company as "proving a real business on time well spent instead of maximizing the total time spent" **[Claimed]** ([TED transcript](https://www.ted.com/podcasts/ted.com/podcasts/fixing-social-media-with-pinterest-ceo-bill-ready-transcript)).
- **Instagram Following / Favorites feeds:** chronological feed of accounts you follow, plus a Favorites-only feed for fast catch-up — offered especially to teens ([Meta Newsroom Nov 2024](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/11/introducing-recommendations-reset-instagram/)). **[Documented]**
- **StoryGraph:** declared-intent survey as the primary rec input (§2.2) — the cleanest "intent-first" precedent in reading.

### 3.4 Explainable recommendations and user tuning controls

- **TikTok "Why this video":** share panel → question-mark icon → plain-language reasons (your interactions, accounts you follow, recently posted in your region, popular in your region) ([TikTok Newsroom, Dec 2022](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/learn-why-a-video-is-recommended-for-you)). TikTok's own framing: "we tried to make the technical details more easily understandable." Coverage notes it "stops short of truly demystifying that black box" ([The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/2022/12/20/23517484/tiktok-recommendation-reason-for-you-page-algorithm)).
- **YouTube:** publishes its ranking signals and explicitly says satisfaction surveys are used "so the system understands satisfaction, not just watch time"; users can give **"Not interested"**, **"Don't recommend channel"** (with "Tell us why"), and can **clear all such feedback**; turning off watch history empties homepage recommendations ([How recommendations work](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16089387), [How YouTube works for you](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9962575), [Manage your recommendations](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6342839)). **[Documented]**
- **Instagram:** "Instagram Ranking Explained" publishes the signal categories and the ~dozen predictions per surface, including that Feed weights "how likely you are to spend a few seconds on a post, comment on it, like it, share it, and tap on the profile photo" — a candid statement that dwell-likelihood is a ranking objective ([Instagram, May 2023](https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/instagram-ranking-explained)). Controls: Interested / Not interested, Hidden Words, Following/Favorites feeds.
- **Goodreads:** plain "how to improve recs" checklist (ratings, genres, shelves, Not interested) (§2.1).

**Pattern:** every major platform now exposes (a) a per-item "why", (b) per-item negative feedback, (c) a channel/author-level block, and (d) in most cases a way to reset the accumulated feedback. None of them expose the objective function itself.

### 3.5 Feed reset and tuning

- **Instagram "Reset suggested content" (tested Nov 2024):** in a few taps, clears personalized recommendations across Explore, Reels and Feed; optionally prompts review/unfollow of frequent sources; re-personalizes from scratch. Adam Mosseri warned: "It's going to make your Instagram much less interesting at first… it's not something I recommend doing all the time — but if you end up in a place where you really don't feel good about your experience, this gives you an out." ([Meta Newsroom](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/11/introducing-recommendations-reset-instagram/), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/11/19/instagram-will-soon-let-you-reset-your-recommendation-algorithm/)). **[Documented]**
- **TikTok "Refresh your For You feed" (Mar 2023):** resets the For You feed to as-if-new-user state (Following feed, profile, inbox unaffected; keyword filters and hidden creators preserved; irreversible) ([TikTok announcement as reported by 9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2023/03/16/tiktok-rolling-out-for-you-feed-reset-button/), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/03/16/tiktoks-new-feature-lets-you-refresh-your-for-you-feed-and-retrain-your-algorithm/)). **[Documented]**
- **TikTok "Manage Topics" (2025):** users dial frequency of 10+ topic categories up/down in the For You feed, alongside keyword filters and an official guide to "understand and shape" the feed; TikTok also says it will "interrupt frequent recommendation of some types of content… such as extreme fitness-related content" — a *frequency-capping on a category* precedent ([TikTok Newsroom, June 2025](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/more-ways-to-discover-new-content-and-creators-you-love)). **[Documented]**
- **YouTube:** per-item feedback + clearable feedback history + watch-history off (§3.4) functions as tuning; no single "reset everything" button found.
- **Readwise Frequency Tuning** (§1.4) — the most granular per-source weight control found anywhere in this survey: user-facing probability weights per document.

### 3.6 Scroll and attention reminders — how they actually behave

- All the reminders above share a design signature: **dismissible, non-blocking, and (except Teen Accounts / TikTok teen defaults) opt-in or default-off.** YouTube's adult default is "never" ([The Verge 2018](https://www.theverge.com/2018/5/11/17346122/youtube-take-a-break-notifications-google-digital-wellbeing) notes they "won't close the app… it's up to you to act on or disregard them").
- Escalation ladder exists at TikTok: first full-screen prompt → second **harder-to-dismiss** prompt (Sleep Hours) → parent passcode for limits ([TikTok Newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/new-ways-we-are-supporting-parents-and-helping-teens-build-balanced-digital-habits)). **[Documented]**

## 4. Evidence review: what worked, what backfired, what is contested

### 4.1 Evidence that interventions do *something* (mostly small effects)

- **[Peer-reviewed]** *Facebook deactivation RCT* (Allcott et al., AER 2020): 4 weeks off Facebook improved a subjective-wellbeing index by **0.09 SD** (life satisfaction +0.12 SD, anxiety +0.10 SD), reduced political polarization and news knowledge, increased offline socializing, and caused persistent post-experiment reductions in use. Authors explicitly note the causal effect is "far smaller than we would have estimated using the correlational approach of much prior literature" — i.e. correlational screen-time claims overstate harm ([AEA article](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/aer.20190658), [working paper PDF](http://web.stanford.edu/~gentzkow/research/facebook.pdf)).
- **[Peer-reviewed]** *Systematic review + meta-analysis of digital self-control tools* (Monge Roffarello & De Russis, TOCHI 2023): tools have a **small-to-medium effect** on reducing time on distracting sources; most shipped tools are self-monitoring (timers, dashboards) and the authors call out the need to confront "the business model of contemporary tech companies" ([ACM DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3571810)).
- **[Peer-reviewed]** *Systematic review of digital self-control interventions* (Biedermann et al., JMIR 2021): **awareness-only interventions (stats dashboards, gentle notifications) were "barely effective"**; notifications only worked when *insistent*; **"sanctions have to be sufficiently difficult to overcome, as they will otherwise be quickly dismissed"**; overall confidence in the literature is low (small samples, short studies); some effects look like novelty effects ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/jcal.12581)).
- **[Peer-reviewed]** *Soft commitment devices* (European Economic Review 2021): randomly encouraging users to adopt iOS Screen Time limits cut daily Facebook time by **~33%** among the treated — **but only for people who were already over-using relative to their own stated ideal; light users *increased* usage after adopting limits** ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0014292121002269)). Commitment devices are not neutral.
- **[Peer-reviewed]** *Wellspent RCT* (JMIR mHealth 2026): user-configurable full-screen reminders (goal, interval, tone, alternative activity) reduced time on the user's most problematic app by **~29 min/day**; **33–34% of reminder events led to stopping within 1 minute**; 78% of participants kept using the tool voluntarily in week 3 ([RCT PDF](https://mhealth.jmir.org/2026/1/e56824/PDF)).
- **[Peer-reviewed, pilot]** *INSTARUPT* (European Journal of Health Psychology 2024): reflective notifications after 3 min of Instagram use **did not significantly reduce measured Instagram/smartphone time**, though self-reported problematic use fell; success rate of closing Instagram within 5 min ranged 25–77% across individuals; reflection-type prompts performed best (54%) ([DOI](https://econtent.hogrefe.com/doi/10.1027/2512-8442/a000150)).
- **[Peer-reviewed, exploratory]** *Awareness nudges for infinite scroll / pull-to-refresh* (Monge Roffarello & De Russis): widgets visualizing scroll intensity reduced *scrolling intensity and compulsive refreshes* and improved perceived control, **but did not change total minutes on app** ([PDF](https://iris.polito.it/retrieve/c23c724f-2463-40d4-98dd-1701d3a8c515/nudging.pdf)). Visibility of the mechanism changes *how* people scroll before it changes *how long*.
- **[Product A/B data — Claimed]** *Duolingo*: a "Weekend Amulet" (letting users skip a day without losing their streak) made learners **4% more likely to return the next week and 5% less likely to lose their streak**; Duolingo's own conclusion: "by giving learners the option to take a break, they're actually more likely to do more in the long run." Streak Wager (+14% day-7 retention) shows the opposite direction — pressure also works ([Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-streaks-keep-duolingo-learners-committed-to-their-language-goals/)). Duolingo's whitepaper describes streaks + notifications as the core retention loop ([whitepaper PDF](https://duolingo-papers.s3.amazonaws.com/reports/Duolingo_whitepaper_duolingo_method_2023.pdf)).
- **[Claimed, platform-reported]** TikTok: prompt to set a screen-time limit raised tool usage **234%**; Instagram: **>90%** of teens who set break reminders keep them on; TikTok: **98%** of teens kept the wind-down meditation on (links in §3.2). All are vendor-reported, measured on adopters (self-selected), not independently audited.

### 4.2 What backfired or has failure modes

1. **Completion states get monetized into continuation.** Instagram's "You're All Caught Up" (2018) became a launchpad for Suggested Posts and ads (2020) — a documented reversal of an anti-doomscroll feature under engagement/ad pressure (§3.1). **[Documented]**
2. **Reminders are routinely dismissed; hard limits provoke reactance.** A 7-day field study (N=104, 2026 preprint) found a plain "take a break" pop-up was *objectively* most effective (especially for low-self-control users) but its subjective acceptability collapsed if not acted on immediately; prior work it cites shows users "frequently dismiss timers, disable reminders, or abandon lockout apps because they feel overly controlled"; too little friction is ignored, too much causes abandonment ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.15818)). **[Contested/early]** (preprint).
3. **Built-in time reminders can lengthen sessions.** A 4-day deployment of three break-reminder designs in a casual game found descriptive data where **time spent was higher with interventions than without**, and players said low limits made them "take the time spent on the game less seriously" and keep extending ([ACM CHI play](https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3532106.3533460)). Small N, exploratory. **[Contested/early]**
4. **Gamified daily-completion pressure correlates with problematic use.** In 2,483 Belgian early adolescents, engaging in Snapchat streaks was associated with higher problematic smartphone use (t=6.22, p<.001); FOMO, problematic use and lower self-control correlated with more/longer streaks — but **effect sizes were small** and the authors caution streaks may largely be "part of a normative communication process" ([Computers in Human Behavior Reports 2023](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2772503023000476)). **[Peer-reviewed, mixed]**
5. **Time-limit nudges can *increase* use in light users** (commitment-device RCT, §4.1). **[Peer-reviewed]**
6. **Opt-in wellbeing tools have structurally low reach:** YouTube's take-a-break reminder is default-off for all adults; Instagram's Take a Break was opt-in; heavy users — the ones most in need — are least likely to enable them. **[Documented]** (defaults in §3.2); the inference about reach is ours.
7. **Feed-composition causally affects expressed emotion — with an ethics overhang.** Facebook's 2014 experiment (N=689,003) reduced emotional content in News Feeds and shifted users' own posting valence — proof that ranking/composition choices have measurable downstream effects on users ([PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4066473/)). PNAS attached an **Editorial Expression of Concern** over informed consent/opt-out ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24994898/)). **[Peer-reviewed but ethically contested]**
8. **Internal deactivation research reportedly found harm and was halted.** Court filings in US school-district litigation allege Meta's 2020 "Project Mercury" study (with Nielsen) found people who stopped using Facebook/Instagram for a week reported **lower depression, anxiety, loneliness and social comparison**, after which the research program was stopped; Meta responds that the study's methodology was flawed (Reuters report, Nov 2025) ([Japan Times/Reuters](https://www.japantimes.co.jp/business/2025/11/23/tech/meta-buried-social-media-harm/)). **[Contested — litigation allegation vs company rebuttal]**

### 4.3 Claims that are genuinely contested (do not treat as settled facts)

- **"Rabbit holes / algorithmic radicalization":** two large studies disagree. Hosseinmardi et al. (PNAS 2021, 300k-user panel) found "no evidence that engagement with far-right content is caused by YouTube recommendations systematically" ([PNAS abstract](https://www.pnas.org/doi/abs/10.1073/pnas.2101967118)); a 100,000-sock-puppet audit (PNAS 2023) found recommendations *do* get more problematic deeper into a trail — but problematic channels never exceed ~2.5% of recommendations, encountered by >32% of puppets ([PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2213020120)); Ribeiro et al. (FAT* 2020) found user migration from milder to more extreme content but could not attribute it to the recommender ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.08313)). **[Contested]**
- **"Doomscrolling" as a construct:** the canonical measure (Sharma, Lee & Johnson, *Technology, Mind, and Behavior* 2022, DOI 10.1037/tmb0000059) is cross-sectional; validation studies find it correlates with distress and lower wellbeing but explicitly call for "longeritudinal and experimental studies" ([Satici et al. 2022/23](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11482-022-10110-7)). Association, not causation. **[Peer-reviewed, correlational]**
- **"Engagement ranking harms wellbeing":** the strongest causal evidence (Allcott 2020) is about *total use*, not ranking objective, and the effect is modest (0.09 SD). Platform internal experiments (Project Mercury) cut both ways depending on who you believe. **[Contested]**
- **Vendor wellbeing metrics** (90% / 98% / 234%): measured by the platforms, often on self-selected adopters, never independently audited. **[Claimed]**

## Implications for the anti-doomscrolling contract decision

Decision-relevant facts and patterns only — the contract itself is ticket #8's to decide.

1. **A completion state is a real, shipped pattern — and a proven fragile one.** Instagram's "You're All Caught Up" (2018) was real, then extended into infinite suggested content in 2020 because "people continue to seek out more posts" ([IG](https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/introducing-youre-all-caught-up-in-feed), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/08/19/instagram-finds-new-ad-space-at-the-end-of-your-feed-with-launch-of-suggested-posts-feature/)). **[Documented]** Whatever BookTube's contract says about a feed ending is only as durable as its resistance to a later "go a little deeper" request.
2. **Defaults, not options, are what platforms actually ship for boundaries.** The reach of every wellbeing feature tracks its default: YouTube break reminders are default-on only for 13–17 ([help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9012523)); Instagram Teen Accounts and TikTok under-18s get *default* 60-minute reminders, sleep/quiet modes and night interruptions ([IG](https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/instagram-teen-accounts), [TikTok](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/new-features-for-teens-and-families-on-tiktok-us)). **[Documented]** Opt-in tools are near-universally criticized as low-reach.
3. **Regulation already mandates an intent/non-profiling feed option for large platforms, and forbids nudging users toward the profiling option.** DSA Art. 38 + EDPB Guidelines 3/2025 ([Art. 38](https://www.digitalacts.eu/regulation/digital-service-act/article/38/recommender-systems), [EDPB PDF](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2025-09/edpb_guidelines_202503_interplay-dsa-gdpr_v1_en.pdf)). **[Documented]** A "Following/intent" mode presented as an equal peer to a personalized mode is becoming a compliance floor, not a differentiator — relevant even to a pre-scale product's contract wording.
4. **Gentle awareness interventions have small-to-medium effects at best, and awareness-only designs are "barely effective."** Meta-analyses: TOCHI 2023 ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3571810)); JMIR 2021 — notifications must be insistent, sanctions must be hard to overcome ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/jcal.12581)). **[Peer-reviewed]** A contract that promises "gentle reminders will keep it healthy" is not supported by the evidence; a contract that specifies *how hard the boundary is to bypass* is addressing the variable the evidence says matters.
5. **Friction has a measurable two-sided failure mode:** too easily dismissed → ignored; too controlling → reactance, disabling, and sometimes *longer* sessions ([arXiv 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.15818), [game study](https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3532106.3533460)). **[Contested/early but consistent]** Also direction-dependent: limits *increased* use for already-light users (EER 2021, [link](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0014292121002269)) **[Peer-reviewed]**. Any contract should say *which* users a boundary targets and what happens when it's overridden.
6. **Explicit-intent inputs outperform behavioural inference at the trust layer.** StoryGraph's founder rejected opaque scores because of documented user "distrust of algorithms," choosing a retakeable free-text preference survey ([roadmap](https://roadmap.thestorygraph.com/requests-ideas/posts/will-i-like-this-book-)); Readwise exposes per-source probability weights over its resurfacing algorithm ([FAQ](https://docs.readwise.io/readwise/docs/faqs/reviewing-highlights)); Bluesky makes the algorithm itself a selectable, inspectable object ([blog](https://bsky.social/about/blog/7-27-2023-custom-feeds)). **[Documented]** None of these required black-box ranking to work commercially.
7. **Reset + explainability is now table stakes and is cheap to copy:** per-item "why this video" (TikTok, [link](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/learn-why-a-video-is-recommended-for-you)), clearable negative feedback (YouTube, [link](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6342839)), full feed reset (Instagram [link](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/11/introducing-recommendations-reset-instagram/), TikTok [link](https://techcrunch.com/2023/03/16/tiktoks-new-feature-lets-you-refresh-your-for-you-feed-and-retrain-your-algorithm/)), topic frequency dials (TikTok Manage Topics, [link](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/more-ways-to-discover-new-content-and-creators-you-love)). **[Documented]** A contract without reset/tuning/explanation clauses would be *behind* current platform baselines.
8. **The category BookTube competes in (book summaries / reading) already models finite, completion-oriented sessions:** 15-minute units (Blinkist, getAbstract), a daily review queue that empties (Readwise), recaps whose purpose is *finishing books* (Storytel), diaries/annual reviews (Letterboxd), and feed flood protection (Letterboxd's 1-item-per-hour backfill throttle, [link](https://letterboxd.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/15179259045903-Why-do-my-reviews-sometimes-not-appear-in-Activity)). **[Documented]** There is no precedent in this category for engagement-optimized infinite scroll, so the "infinite discovery is allowed" requirement has no in-category benchmark to inherit from.
9. **Harm claims that motivate the whole project are correlational or contested at the causal level.** Doomscrolling scales are cross-sectional ([link](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11482-022-10110-7)); rabbit-hole evidence conflicts ([PNAS 2021](https://www.pnas.org/doi/abs/10.1073/pnas.2101967118) vs [PNAS 2023](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2213020120)); the best causal estimate of leaving a feed is modest (+0.09 SD wellbeing, [AER 2020](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/aer.20190658)). **[Contested]** A contract justified as "preventing documented harm" is on weaker ground than one justified as "protecting a stated product value and user agency" — which is a positioning fact for ticket #8 to weigh, not a recommendation.
10. **Vendor-reported wellbeing metrics are not evidence.** 90% (Meta), 98% and 234% (TikTok) are platform-reported on adopters ([links in §3.2](#32-session-boundaries-usage-controls-and-wellbeing-modes)). **[Claimed]** Any BookTube contract that cites targets should assume such numbers will be its own to produce and audit.
